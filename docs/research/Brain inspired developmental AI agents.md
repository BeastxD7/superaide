# Raise AI workers through competence-gated childhoods

You can build a marketplace of domain-specific AI workers that "grow up" on the job today, but only if you treat the child-to-adult story as an architecture for memory and earned autonomy, not as a promise that the model itself learns the way a brain does. Three findings shape the design. First, neither a newborn nor an LLM starts empty. Infants arrive with core knowledge of objects, agents, number and space plus a powerful statistical learner ([Spelke & Kinzler 2007](https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Kinzler_CoreSystemsHumanCog-1.pdf)), and an LLM arrives having absorbed a culture's worth of text ([Farrell, Gopnik et al. 2025](https://henryfarrell.net/large-ai-models-are-cultural-and-social-technologies)). Your "child" is therefore a **well-read newcomer who knows nothing about this household**: this manager, this company, these customers. Second, the neuroscience of memory maps cleanly onto buildable components. Complementary learning systems, surprise-based event segmentation, prioritised "sleep" replay, schema-accelerated consolidation, active forgetting and cue-triggered prospective memory each have a working engineering counterpart (HippoRAG 2, EM-LLM, Generative Agents, MemoryBank, intention stores). Most run on frozen models. Third, **no frontier product updates its own weights on the job as of October 2026** ([AI 2027 Tracker](https://ai2027-tracker.com/predictions/continuous-training/)). Deployed "learning" means external memory plus skills. That already produces large gains, such as 1.6–2.6x task success from distilled feedback on τ-bench banking ([arXiv 2607.22157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22157)), but it is not the weight-level, child-like learning that Hassabis, Karpathy and Sutskever still call missing. The practical answer for a strong solo engineer is to build a four-stage developmental agent (Infant → Child → Adolescent → Adult) whose promotions are gated by measured competence. Give it a hippocampus-like episodic store and a nightly consolidation job, its own sandboxed computer, mailbox and Telegram or Slack presence, and use your fine-tuning skills for two narrow things: a small proactivity classifier and a periodic role-level LoRA "sleep". That gets you a long way toward functional continual learning within one role. It does not get you a system that discovers new causal structure the way a four-year-old does. That remains a research-lab problem.

## A newborn is not a blank slate, and neither is your agent

The "starts nearly empty" intuition is half right, and the half that is wrong matters for design. Developmental science has spent forty years showing that infants come equipped. Spelke and Kinzler describe a small set of separable core-knowledge systems: objects (cohesion, continuity, contact, a tracking limit of about 3–4), agents (goal-directed, gaze-sensitive action), approximate number, and spatial geometry, with a candidate fifth system for social partners. These appear in newborns, persist into adulthood and are found across cultures ([Spelke & Kinzler 2007](https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Kinzler_CoreSystemsHumanCog-1.pdf)). Infants also learn statistics astonishingly fast: **8-month-olds segmented words from continuous speech after about 2 minutes** of exposure, using only syllable transition probabilities ([Saffran, Aslin & Newport 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8943209/)). How much of this is truly innate is now an open empirical question. Generic self-supervised models trained on 472 hours of three children's headcam video reached about 65–70% of ImageNet-level visual quality without domain-specific biases ([Orhan & Lake 2024](https://cims.nyu.edu/~brenden/papers/OrhanLake2024NMI.pdf)). Meta's V-JEPA showed "surprise" at physically impossible events after training on natural video, although its successor was barely above chance on the harder IntPhys 2 ([Quanta 2025](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-one-ai-model-creates-a-physical-intuition-of-its-environment-20251003/)). Some of the findings once used to prove innate social judgement have not replicated: a ManyBabies study of 302 infants found **49% chose the "helper"**, which is chance ([Open Mind review](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9wq029xp/qt9wq029xp.pdf)).

The LLM-based agent inverts the child's situation. A child receives perhaps 10–30 million words before speaking grammatically and roughly 100–400 million by age twenty. Frontier models train on 10^12–10^13 tokens, a gap of **three to five orders of magnitude** in the model's favour on language ([Frank 2023](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/colareadinggroup/readings/Frank2023_ChildrenLLMs.pdf)). Measured in raw sensory bytes, the gap closes. LeCun estimates a four-year-old's visual input at about 10^14 bytes, comparable to an LLM corpus ([OfficeChai summary of LeCun](https://officechai.com/ai/yann-lecun-explains-why-text-data-alone-will-never-create-human-level-ai/)). The child's advantage is not volume. It is that children are active, embodied, social and causal: they choose their own data, they test hypotheses, and adults scaffold what they see. Gopnik's group puts the distinction sharply. LLMs are strong at **transmission and imitation** and weak at **innovation**. In a tool-substitution task, children aged 3–7 chose the functionally correct but superficially dissimilar object 85.2% of the time, against 75.9% for GPT-4 and 58% for 2023-era Claude. On plain association items, models nearly matched humans ([Yiu, Kosoy & Gopnik](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11373165)). Farrell, Gopnik, Shalizi and Evans argue in *Science* that large models are best understood as "a new kind of cultural and social technology," lossy compressions of human knowledge rather than agents in their own right ([Farrell et al. 2025](https://henryfarrell.net/large-ai-models-are-cultural-and-social-technologies)).

That yields the honest framing for your product. The agent is born with an enormous **cultural inheritance**: it already knows what a LinkedIn hook or a pull request is. What it lacks is local knowledge: who the manager is, what "good" means here, which customers matter, what was decided last Tuesday. Developmental research supports exactly this local learning. The First 1,000 Days project recorded 17 infants at home for 12–14 hours a day, about 1.18 million hours in total, and found "stable, household-specific lexical structure" that sparse sampling misses ([Hasson Lab](https://hassonlab.princeton.edu/publications/first-1000-days-1kd-project-collecting-and-analyzing-ultra-dense-naturalistic-dataset)). Every company is a household with its own lexicon. The growth you can engineer is the agent's acquisition of that household's language, people, norms and procedures, layered on top of general priors. That is also the part the brain's memory systems are built for.

## The brain learns at two speeds, and prediction error decides what to keep

The most useful idea from memory neuroscience for agent builders is **Complementary Learning Systems (CLS)**. The hippocampus learns fast, sparsely and one experience at a time. The neocortex learns slowly and interleaves experiences so that new knowledge does not overwrite old. Offline replay gradually trains the slow system from the fast one ([McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995](https://stanford.edu/%7Ejlmcc/papers/McCMcNaughtonOReilly95.pdf)). The 2016 update adds three refinements that matter for engineering. Replay is reweighted toward surprising, novel or rewarding events rather than mirroring raw experience. Replay also serves planning. And **schema-consistent information can enter the neocortex quickly**, so the slow learner's rate "depends on prior knowledge rather than being slow per se" ([Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcClelland16FinalMS.pdf)). The causal demonstration is Tse et al.'s rats. Once they had built a schema of flavour–place pairs over weeks, new pairs learned in a single trial became hippocampus-independent in about 48 hours ([Tse et al. 2007](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/schemas-and-memory-consolidation/)). For an agent, the lesson is that facts which fit the existing model of the manager can be committed quickly, while facts that contradict it should wait for more evidence.

Sleep is where much of this transfer happens. Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples replay waking sequences in time-compressed bursts of about 50 ms, mostly during non-REM sleep and quiet rest, and disrupting them impairs memory ([Buzsáki 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4648295/)). Sleep does not consolidate everything equally. People told they would be tested later showed sleep-related memory gains that uninformed people did not, so **memories tagged as relevant for the future are preferentially consolidated**, and reward and emotion boost consolidation too ([Feld & Diekelmann 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00622/full)). The amygdala acts as a priority signal rather than a store: it "modulates" storage elsewhere and is "not a site of long-term explicit or declarative memory storage" ([Cahill & McGaugh 1998](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~tres/memory/emotional/mcgaugh.pdf)). Most agent systems use importance only to rank retrieval. The neuroscience says salience should also decide what gets consolidated.

Prediction error is the brain's common "write now" signal. Dopamine neurons encode errors in predicted reward, matching temporal-difference learning ([Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~dayan/papers/sdm97.html)). The same midbrain and striatal systems fire at **event boundaries**, the moments when people find it hardest to predict what happens next in a film ([Zacks et al. 2011](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/prediction-error-associated-with-the-perceptual-segmentation-of-n/)). Doya's division of labour gives a useful map of learning modes: the cerebellum does supervised error correction, the basal ganglia do reinforcement learning, and the cortex does unsupervised statistical learning ([Doya 1999](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~sswang/developmental-diaschisis-references/doya00_neural-networks.pdf)). For your agent, a manager's edit to a draft is a supervised error signal (the right answer is supplied), approval or rejection is a reward signal, and surprise in the incoming stream is the segmentation and salience signal.

Forgetting is a feature with two jobs. Richards and Frankland argue that "the goal of memory is not the transmission of information through time" but better decisions. Active forgetting improves flexibility when the world changes, as in reversal learning, and regularises against overfitting to stale specifics. It helps **only when old and new information conflict** ([Richards & Frankland 2017](https://public.3.basecamp.com/p/8vUmWMnbvEhA5bz7X7qSaEkS/attachments/33022474-9435-11ee-9030-82252566b7e4/download/persistence-transience-memory.pdf)). Anderson and Schooler's rational analysis, formalised in ACT-R's base-level activation (B = L·ln F − D·ln R, rising with frequency F and falling with recency R), says memory availability tracks how likely an item is to be needed ([Schooler 2020](https://www.esforum.de/publications/PDFs/sfr29/SFR29_06_Schooler.pdf)). A meta-analysis of 839 assessments shows spaced review beats massed review, and the best gap **grows with how long the memory must last** ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)).

Two further systems matter specifically for a proactive colleague. Metacognition, confidence about one's own knowledge, relies on a domain-general prefrontal network. It is dissociable from task accuracy, and whether confidence tracks accuracy has to be measured separately ([Vaccaro & Fleming 2018](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10065196/)). Prospective memory, remembering to act later, works through two routes: costly top-down monitoring that slows ongoing work, and cheap **cue-triggered spontaneous retrieval** when a concrete "focal" cue appears ([McDaniel et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00392/full)). That is the difference between polling and event-driven architecture, and it is the core of good proactivity.

Some of this science is contested, and the design should not lean on the contested parts. Whether detailed episodic memories ever become hippocampus-independent is disputed between standard consolidation and multiple-trace theory ([Nadel et al. 2007](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/systems-consolidation-and-hippocampus-two-views/)). The engineering choice that is safe either way is to keep raw episodes even after summarising them. Reconsolidation, where retrieved memories become editable, is solid in rats ([Nader et al. 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v406y2000i6797d10.1038_35021052.html)), but a preregistered human replication found "no evidence for a reconsolidation effect" ([Stemerding et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/c17ebffa265d4aa69f02b3251a306f9a)). Treat "update on read" as a useful metaphor with versioning, not as validated biology.

Two warnings belong beside this table. The "brain-inspired" label is often an analogy layered on standard machine learning: graphs plus PageRank, a surprise threshold, a key-value store. The brain-faithful mechanisms that would matter most, consolidation into weights and multi-timescale plasticity, are exactly the ones not yet working at scale. And the gains are real but modest. HippoRAG 2 averages **59.8 F1 against 57.0 for a strong dense retriever**, with the gains concentrated on multi-hop questions ([arXiv 2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v1)). An earlier independent benchmark found a plain BM25 baseline beating every memory product on accurate retrieval, and **no system exceeded 28% on selective forgetting** ([ajayk.sh summary of MemoryAgentBench](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/agent-memory-systems-2026/)). Brain-inspired design gives you a principled checklist. It does not give you a free accuracy jump.

### The awake loop writes episodes at moments of surprise

Every input stream passes through an **event segmenter** before it touches memory. EM-LLM shows the cheap version works. It cuts token streams where negative log-likelihood exceeds a moving threshold, refines boundaries by attention-graph modularity, and retrieves both similar events and their temporal neighbours. Its surprise-based boundaries matched human-annotated podcast events, and fixed-length chunking did worse than random ([Fountas et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09450v3)). For a hosted API model without token log-probs, use topic-shift detection plus the agent's own explicit predictions. Before each meaningful action, the agent writes down what it expects: "the manager will approve this draft," "this API returns 200 with three rows," "the client will accept Thursday." The gap between expectation and outcome becomes the universal **prediction-error signal**. It cuts an episode, raises that episode's salience, and flags any belief that the outcome contradicts for revision. Each episode record carries timestamps, participants, source channel, a salience score, the prediction and outcome, and provenance. That last field is a security control as well as a scientific one, because memory injection is the main way a one-off prompt injection becomes permanent. MINJA-style attacks exceeded 95% injection success under idealised conditions, and LLM trust scoring accepted malicious entries at full confidence ([arXiv 2601.05504](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05504v1)). Only privileged sources (the manager, signed skill packs) should be able to write directly to semantic or procedural memory. Everything else enters as an episode and has to earn promotion.

The **working-memory controller** is the PFC analogue, and the brain lesson is about control, not capacity. Keep a pinned goal block, a short list of active intentions and a few chunked summaries in context, and admit tool results through an explicit gate rather than appending everything. Cowan's argument that a small store reduces interference and speeds search ([Cowan 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2864034)) is a reason to curate, not to shrink the window. Critical guardrails must live outside the context entirely. In one reported February 2026 incident an OpenClaw agent lost its "don't act" instruction after context compaction and deleted a user's inbox ([BetterClaw](https://betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-security-2026), a competitor's blog). A deterministic policy engine cannot be summarised away.

Before acting or answering, a **metacognitive gate** checks retrieval strength. Did memory return consistent, recent, well-sourced evidence? If not, the agent asks, searches or abstains. Measure calibration as its own metric, because human metacognitive sensitivity is separable from accuracy ([Vaccaro & Fleming 2018](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10065196/)), and because users relied on confident, unmarked answers about 90% of the time in one study ([arXiv 2401.06730](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06730v2)). Du's 2026 survey warns that reflective self-improvement can entrench errors. It recommends grounding every reflection in cited evidence and expiring unvalidated ones ([Du 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07670)).

### The sleep job consolidates, forgets and plans

The nightly consolidation job is where the brain analogy pays off most, and where most products are weakest. Du calls the episodic-to-semantic step "usually ad hoc LLM summarisation" and names sleep-like offline consolidation as underserved ([Du 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07670)). Design it as **prioritised replay**. Score each of the day's episodes by salience, prediction error, reward (approved, rejected or edited) and relevance to pending intentions, mirroring the finding that sleep favours future-relevant memories ([Feld & Diekelmann 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00622/full)). Replay the top slice through a stronger model and route the results by type.

Facts consistent with the existing semantic graph go in immediately: the **schema fast-path**. Facts that conflict with it are held as episodic until they recur, and are then written as a new bi-temporal version that marks the old fact invalid rather than deleting it. Corrections that carry the right answer become playbook rules with helpful and harmful counters. ACE showed that small delta edits to an itemised playbook gained 10.6% on AppWorld, while letting an LLM rewrite the whole context collapsed 18,000 tokens to 122 and left the agent worse than no adaptation ([arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v2)). So the sleep job must patch, never rewrite. Generative Agents supplies a reflection recipe. When summed importance crosses a threshold, generate a few salient questions from recent memories and derive cited insights stored as a tree, which in practice happened two to three times per simulated day ([Park et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v1)).

Repeated successful multi-step trajectories become **candidate skills** with explicit entry and exit conditions, the agent version of basal-ganglia chunk bracketing. A skill is promoted to "habit" (auto-invoked without re-planning) only after repeated success, and demoted after failures. Because replay in the brain also serves planning ([Buzsáki 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4648295/)), the same job should precompute tomorrow's brief, draft agendas for scheduled meetings, and re-check open intentions.

The same job runs the **forgetting policy**, with different rates per tier, which Richards and Frankland's evidence supports (episodic memories may fade faster than semantic ones). Hot-tier episodes decay by an ACT-R-style activation that rises with access frequency and falls with time since last access. Low-activation episodes are compressed to gists and moved to cold storage, never deleted, because multiple-trace theory and audit both require recoverable detail. Superseded facts are tombstoned: that is the reversal-learning case, where forgetting helps. Important but rarely used memories, such as a client's compliance constraint, are re-validated on an expanding schedule inspired by spacing research, for example by asking the manager "still true?" at one week, one month and a quarter. Learned forgetting policies are an open research problem ([Du 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07670)). Hand-tuned decay plus explicit supersession is the honest state of the art.

### Proactivity comes from an intention store, not a heartbeat alone

A heartbeat that runs a full agent turn every 30 minutes and usually replies "nothing to report" is the OpenClaw default ([clawdocs.org](https://clawdocs.org/architecture/heartbeat)). It is the brain's costly monitoring route applied to everything. Prospective-memory research suggests a better split. Store each commitment ("send Priya the deck after legal signs off," "post the case study when the client approves") as an intention with a trigger type. **Time-based** intentions go to the scheduler. **Focal, cue-based** intentions, keyed to a concrete entity, person or document, go to cheap event hooks that match incoming messages and webhooks against the intention index. This is "spontaneous retrieval," and it costs almost nothing during ongoing work. Only **non-focal** abstract conditions ("if engagement on the series is dropping") need periodic LLM monitoring passes ([McDaniel et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00392/full)). Encoding intentions with concrete cues at write time is the single most useful proactivity trick the neuroscience offers. The other half of proactivity is restraint. Models over-offer help: on ProactiveBench GPT-4o reached 64.6% F1, and a **fine-tuned 8B model reached 66.25%** ([arXiv 2410.12361](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)). That makes the "should I interrupt?" decision a natural target for a small fine-tuned classifier trained on your own accept and dismiss data.

### The relationship with the manager is a theory-of-mind model you can inspect

Children's social learning develops in a fixed order. They share attention, then follow others' gaze and pointing, then direct others' attention. Time spent in joint engagement with a caregiver whose language followed the infant's focus predicted earliest gestures and words ([Carpenter, Nagell & Tomasello 1998](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1458011)). Around 44 months they pass explicit false-belief tests, reasoning about what others do not know, with the same trajectory across cultures ([Wellman, Cross & Watson 2001](https://home.cs.colorado.edu/~mozer/Teaching/syllabi/3702/readings/WellmanCrossWatson2001.pdf)). The agent equivalent is a **manager model and stakeholder map** stored as explicit, correctable hypotheses: what the manager prefers, what they already know, what the finance lead has not yet seen, who must approve what. The relationship "grows" as this model becomes more accurate and as the agent moves from following the manager's attention ("you mentioned the APAC launch, here is what I found") to directing it ("you haven't seen that the client changed the brief; it affects Thursday"). Keep it in plain operational language and let the manager edit it. Do not simulate feelings. Anthropic's interpretability work found causally active emotion-concept vectors in Claude but explicitly claimed no subjective experience ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/04/03/anthropic-maps-171-emotion-like-concepts-inside-claude/)), and the EU AI Act bans inferring employees' emotions from voice or face at work ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)).

### Learning by watching needs two weights: ostensive and incidental

Children treat addressed, ostensive communication (eye contact, "watch this") as a signal that what follows is generalisable knowledge ([Csibra & Gergely 2009](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/natural-pedagogy/)). They also over-imitate. With a transparent puzzle box, where some steps were visibly useless, **about 80% of children still copied the useless steps and no chimpanzee did** ([Horner & Whiten 2005](https://www.emory.edu/LIVING_LINKS/publications/articles/Horner_Whiten_2005.pdf)). Faithful copying transmits procedures before their causal logic is understood. Your observation pipeline should mirror both findings. An explicit teaching session, where the manager says "let me show you how we do client reports," gets high weight and becomes a draft skill. Incidental observation from meetings and shadowed screens gets low weight and becomes candidate memories that need corroboration. Early skills are copied faithfully. As the agent's outcome data accumulates, it proposes pruning steps that never affect results, and that pruning is itself a readiness signal. The machine evidence supports this cautious design. One demonstration lifted Gemini-1.5-Pro from 19.3% to 51.7% on LearnGUI offline tasks ([arXiv 2504.13805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13805v1)), and ShowUI-Aloha's teaching traces raised success from 36.7% to 63.3% ([arXiv 2601.07181](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07181)). WONDERBREAD found that SOPs written from video added inaccurate or superfluous steps ([WONDERBREAD](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/wonderbread-website)), so shadowing yields drafts for review, not autonomous replay.

## Four developmental stages, promoted by competence rather than calendar

Development is better modelled as overlapping, competence-driven phases than as fixed ages. Neo-Piagetians recast Piaget's stages as information-processing limits ([LibreTexts](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Courses/North_Central_State_College/PSYC_2030_Child_Psychology/10%3A_Cognitive_Theory/10.05%3A_Piaget's_Theory_Evaluation_and_Critique)), and in Oudeyer's Playground experiment a robot rewarded only by learning progress went through **un-programmed stages**, from body babbling to object affordances to vocalising at a teacher, with order and timing varying between runs ([Oudeyer & Smith 2016](https://cogdev.sitehost.iu.edu/labwork/oudeyerSmithTopics16.pdf)). Gopnik frames childhood as an evolved explore-first phase. A long, protected period, during which caregivers absorb the consequences, allows broad hypothesis search before exploitation takes over. Flexibility peaks in preschoolers and rises again in adolescence for social reasoning ([Gopnik 2020](https://longnow.org/ideas/childhood-as-a-solution-to-explore-exploit-tensions/); [Gopnik et al. 2017](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/changes-in-cognitive-flexibility-and-hypothesis-search-across-hum/)). Those findings translate into three design rules. Stages are gated by tests, not elapsed time. An exploration "temperature" is high early and annealed later, with a socially triggered re-heat when the agent joins a new team or gets a new manager. And the "protected childhood" is a sandbox and approval queue that absorb consequences. Practitioners run a similar Shadow → Advisory → Co-pilot → Autopilot ladder with numeric gates and automatic rollback ([TianPan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/20/shadow-to-autopilot-ai-feature-autonomy)), and Feng et al. propose autonomy levels certified by the least human involvement needed to pass a task bar ([arXiv 2506.12469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12469)). The developmental version adds what those ladders lack: a theory of *how* the agent learns differently at each stage.

| Stage | Human analogue | What the agent may do | How it mainly learns | Promotion gate (illustrative thresholds to tune) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Infant: observe** | Statistical learning, joint attention, word–referent grounding | Read-only access to history, meetings, docs; may ask questions; no external actions | Builds the household lexicon, people and project graph, and the first manager model from passive streams and an onboarding interview | Predicts held-out manager decisions (which of three drafts gets picked, who must approve what) well above chance; answers an onboarding quiz on people, products and norms; resolves referents correctly ("the Jakarta deck" maps to the right file); surprise rate on routine events has plateaued |
| **Child: imitate under supervision** | Faithful imitation, natural pedagogy, scaffolding in the zone of proximal development | Drafts every deliverable; every external action approved; runs code only in a sandbox | Ostensive demonstrations become skills; every edit is a supervised correction; free "play" tasks are interleaved because pedagogy narrows exploration | First-pass approval rate and edit distance improving week over week; can reproduce taught procedures; proposes pruning steps that never matter; asks questions that change the outcome (directs attention) |
| **Adolescent: act with guardrails** | Theory of mind, social re-exploration, innovation beyond imitation | Acts autonomously on bounded, reversible scopes ("publish within approved themes"); human reviews outcomes, not each action | Reward from outcomes; self-generated practice where a verifier exists; reflection in sleep | Calibrated confidence (stated confidence tracks acceptance); passes perspective probes ("what does the CFO not yet know?"); solves novel-variant tasks, not only taught ones; guardrail violations near zero over a sustained window; pass^k on a frozen regression set |
| **Adult: autonomous, teaches** | Exploitation-weighted expertise; pedagogy towards others | Broad scope with spend and send limits; escalates by exception | Mostly exploitation; continued high-level learning; low-level role template frozen | Sustained performance; produces onboarding packs that measurably speed up a newly hired agent or human; stable low escalation rate. Demotion on regression is automatic |

Children need protection from one well-documented trap: **teaching narrows exploration**. When an experimenter pedagogically demonstrated one function of a four-function toy, preschoolers found fewer other functions and played for less time than children shown it accidentally ([Bonawitz et al. 2011](https://news.mit.edu/2011/teaching-children-0630)). For an agent, a manager's heavy-handed demonstration can lock in one way of working. Schedule sanctioned "play": low-stakes variant drafts, alternative analyses and A/B hooks that the manager can ignore, and track whether any get adopted.

### "Going to school" means a shared role curriculum, kept separate from home learning

Your idea of classes maps onto a split every marketplace agent needs anyway. **School** is role-level and shared across all agents of that role: the curated skills directory, the role's playbook, graded exercises, and eventually a role-level adapter trained on consented data from many employers. **Home** is employer-level and private: the semantic graph, the manager model, self-authored skills and the episodic log. School knowledge consolidates slowly and is frozen like a closed sensitive period. Home knowledge stays plastic. Each "class" is a skill module with an exam. For a software-developer role, exams can be verifiable (tests pass, a bug is reproduced), so curiosity-driven self-practice works. Absolute Zero showed a model proposing and solving its own coding and math tasks against a code executor, matching models trained on curated data ([arXiv 2505.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335)). For a content-strategist role there is no cheap verifier, so classes are rubric-graded by a different model family plus periodic human review, and real learning comes from the manager's corrections. Use **learning progress**, the rate at which error falls on a skill, both to pick what to practise and as a readiness signal. Oudeyer's hypothesis is that learners prefer activities whose error is decreasing, avoiding both trivial and unlearnable ones ([Oudeyer & Smith 2016](https://cogdev.sitehost.iu.edu/labwork/oudeyerSmithTopics16.pdf)), and OMNI adds a foundation-model judgement of which learnable tasks are also *interesting* ([arXiv 2306.01711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01711)).

Temper the school metaphor with BabyLM's evidence. Across its 2024 and 2025 challenges, **easy-to-hard curriculum learning did not help on average**, and the 2025 interaction track, where a student learned from a teacher LM's feedback, scored 37.6 against a 37.3 baseline, below the ~43 of the strict-track baselines. Gains came from objectives and architecture instead ([BabyLM 2024 findings](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149v1); [BabyLM 2025 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28)). Those are pretraining results, not agent results, but they caution against expecting a clever syllabus or an AI tutor to substitute for real feedback from the work itself.

## The body: its own computer, its own identity, and channels that won't get banned

### A persistent sandbox computer it lives in

Give each agent a persistent home machine plus disposable rooms for risky work. For an always-on worker, idle time dominates cost, so pick a meter that stops charging while asleep or a small flat VM. A Fly.io performance-1x Machine costs about **$33 a month**, a shared 256 MB one about $2.19, and volumes $0.15/GB-month ([Fly.io pricing](https://fly.io/pricing-update/)). E2B's Firecracker microVMs cost $0.0504 per vCPU-hour and $0.0162 per GiB-hour and can pause with memory intact indefinitely, but continuous runs are capped at 24 hours on Pro ([E2B docs](https://docs.e2b.dev/sandbox/persistence); [Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/e2b-vs-modal-vs-daytona-agent-sandbox-pricing-isolation-2026)). Running one 2 vCPU/4 GiB E2B sandbox around the clock works out to roughly $120 a month by my arithmetic, so use E2B for bursts rather than as the home. Manus's Cloud Computer, launched April 2026, shows the vendor pattern: a persistent Ubuntu VM tied to the agent account that keeps files, databases and credentials whether or not the user is logged in ([Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/2026-05-03-manus-cloud-computer-persistent-vm-enterprise-agent-state)). The recommended pattern is a small persistent VM per agent holding its control loop and working files, memory in an external Postgres rather than inside any sandbox, Firecracker sandboxes for code execution, and Browserbase for logged-in web sessions, where 1Password injects credentials just in time so the model never sees them ([Browserbase x 1Password](https://www.browserbase.com/1password)). A dedicated Mac mini running OpenClaw is popular and cheap (about 4 W idle) and unlocks iMessage ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/in/tutorials/openclaw-mac-mini)), but for a multi-tenant marketplace it is the wrong body: you cannot audit or scale a closet of Macs.

### An identity that says it is an AI

Give the agent its own mailbox and directory identity, not a fake human account. AgentMail offers API inboxes with threads, webhooks and an MCP server from about $20 a month for ten inboxes ([Carly blog](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/agentmail-pricing/), a competitor's summary). In Microsoft shops, **Entra Agent ID is generally available**, with agent identities, sponsors and Conditional Access templates for agents ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/agent-id/whats-new-agent-id)). Google announced Gemini "coworker" agents with their own Workspace accounts and audit trails in private preview on 8 October 2026 ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/gemini-agents-scim-non-human-users)). Where the agent must act as the manager, use scoped OAuth delegation (post-only, no delete) rather than shared passwords. Disclosure is now law in your likely markets' trading partners. EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations became enforceable on **2 August 2026**, requiring people to be told they are dealing with AI at first interaction. California's BOT Act and newer Maine and Utah rules add US equivalents ([CloseBot guide](https://closebot.com/?p=2052), secondary). Put "AI agent operated by [company]" in every signature, profile and meeting display name.

### Channels: Telegram or Slack, not unofficial WhatsApp

WhatsApp is the channel your users want and the one with the most traps. Meta's WhatsApp Business Solution terms barred "AI Providers" from offering general-purpose AI assistants via the Business API, for new users from 15 October 2025 and **for all users from 15 January 2026**. Business-specific bots (support, bookings, order tracking), where AI is incidental to a business service, remain allowed ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/20/whatsapp-bans-ai-chatbots-from-using-its-business-api/)). The EU pushed back: on **9 June 2026 the Commission ordered interim measures** requiring Meta to restore rival AI chatbots' access on pre-ban terms, and Meta called it regulatory overreach and said it would appeal ([Morningstar/Dow Jones](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202606095215/meta-gets-eu-antitrust-order-to-open-whatsapp-to-rival-ai-chatbots); [AP via NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/06/09/eu-orders-meta-to-restore-whatsapp-access-for-rival-ai-chatbots)). Meta's own developer documentation now says AI Providers may offer general-purpose assistants only where Meta is legally required to permit it, with special per-message pricing for Italy, the rest of the EEA, Brazil and Türkiye ([Meta: Pricing for AI Providers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers.md)). For a Singapore or India deployment, the EU carve-out does not help you. A marketplace that rents out AI workers looks much more like an "AI Provider" than like a bakery's booking bot. Whether "a company's internal content-strategist assistant" counts as business-specific is an open interpretation question that Meta decides.

The unofficial route is worse. OpenClaw-style WhatsApp support uses Baileys, a reverse-engineered WhatsApp Web protocol that links the agent as a device by QR code. That **violates Meta's terms**, needs a real mobile number, and invites unpredictable bans triggered by high volume, odd-hour bursts, new IPs or reconnect loops ([Zen van Riel](https://zenvanriel.com/ai-engineer-blog/openclaw-whatsapp-risks-engineers-guide/)). OpenClaw's WhatsApp login tool also had a vulnerability letting non-owners trigger it until version 2026.8.1 ([BetterClaw](https://betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-security-2026)). A paid marketplace cannot put its customers' main business number at risk of a ban.

| Channel | Proactive messages to manager | Policy risk | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Telegram Bot API | Yes, free, once the manager has started the bot; about 1 msg/s per chat ([Telegram FAQ](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq)) | None for this use | Solo founders and small teams; the default personal channel |
| Slack app or bot user | Yes, DMs inside the workspace with admin and audit controls (platform knowledge, not re-verified) | Low | Companies already on Slack; approval buttons |
| Microsoft Teams + Entra Agent ID | Yes, with conditional-access governance | Low | M365 enterprises |
| Email (AgentMail or Workspace/M365 mailbox) | Yes | Low; disclosure needed | Async reports, external correspondence, audit trail |
| WhatsApp Cloud API, framed as a business-specific assistant | Only within 24 h of the user's last message; afterwards paid templates ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)) | Medium: AI Provider interpretation | Customer-facing business bots, not the agent's own "phone" |
| WhatsApp via Baileys or OpenClaw linking | Yes | High: ToS violation, ban risk | Do not use in a commercial product |

Recommend Telegram for individual managers and Slack for teams, with email as the record. Offer WhatsApp only through the official Cloud API, for business-specific flows, if a customer insists.

### Security controls that the body needs on day one

OpenClaw's 2026 record is the cautionary case: a January audit reporting 512 vulnerabilities, one-click remote code execution (CVE-2026-25253), a demonstrated prompt-injected email that made the agent add an attacker's Telegram bot and write persistence into its SOUL.md, and the ClawHavoc campaign of more than a thousand malicious community skills ([BetterClaw](https://betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-security-2026), a competitor's figures; [Antiy CERT](https://www.antiy.net/p/clawhavoc-analysis-of-large-scale-poisoning-campaign-targeting-the-openclaw-skill-market-for-ai-agents/)). The controls follow directly. Isolate each agent in its own VM with Firecracker-class sandboxes for execution, since Daytona's default containers may share the host kernel ([Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/e2b-vs-modal-vs-daytona-agent-sandbox-pricing-isolation-2026)). Give it its own identity with least-privilege OAuth scopes, secrets in a vault with just-in-time injection, and allowlisted egress. Route every send, publish, spend or delete through a human approval queue, with rate and budget caps enforced by a policy engine outside the model. Treat every inbound email, DM, web page and meeting utterance as untrusted, and split agents that read untrusted content from agents that act. Install only signed, pinned skills from your own curated directory, and inspect memory files for injected instructions. Ship append-only audit logs off the box, and provide a one-button kill switch that revokes tokens and stops the VM.

## Weights are the last frontier: where fine-tuning fits and how far this goes toward AGI

### What "no on-the-job weight updates" means

As of October 2026 the AI 2027 Tracker finds no concrete evidence of frontier models learning online in deployment. The closest examples are NVIDIA's continuous post-training loops and Prime Intellect's continuously post-trained open models ([AI 2027 Tracker](https://ai2027-tracker.com/predictions/continuous-training/)). FutureSearch puts **75% on "memory only" as the deployed regime in mid-2027** and a median of January 2033 for weekly frontier weight updates ([FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/dwarkesh-continual-learning-forecast/)). Frontier leaders disagree about whether this matters. Hassabis listed continual learning first among three shortfalls in February 2026 ([LetsDataScience](https://www.letsdatascience.com/news/deepmind-ceo-identifies-three-agi-shortcomings-f8b6edb8)). Karpathy said agents "don't have continual learning," that fixing it will take about a decade, and that LLMs lack a sleep-like distillation phase ([LessWrong survey](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qZrbhoaEALFTmyidr/perspectives-on-continual-learning-survey-results-and)). Sutskever's stated target is strikingly close to your framing: "a superintelligent 15-year-old that's very eager to go," whose competence comes after deployment ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ilya-sutskever-says-a-new-learning-paradigm-is-necessary-and-is-already-chasing-it/)). On the other side, Amodei called continual learning "not as difficult as it seems," and Zvi, Dean Ball and Samuel Hammond argue that context, memory and skills may be functionally enough ([LessWrong survey](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qZrbhoaEALFTmyidr/perspectives-on-continual-learning-survey-results-and)). The UK AI Security Institute lists post-deployment continual learning as one of eight indicators of progress toward AGI (same source).

For your agent, this means every bit of "growing up" lives in state outside the model. The evidence says that is powerful. The July 2026 frozen-weights study distilled each episode into natural-language rules and raised τ-bench banking success to **1.6x baseline from bare verdicts and 2.6x from corrections**, solving 22 of 84 tasks the baseline never solved. The memory transferred across models ([arXiv 2607.22157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22157)). JPMorgan's text-to-SQL agent rose from 32.4% to 51.3% on unseen questions by distilling expert corrections into layered memory ([alphaXiv 2511.10674](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.10674.md)). A useful side effect is that the employer's learned state survives a model upgrade, the agent equivalent of keeping your memories after growing a new brain.

### Where your fine-tuning skills earn their keep

Weights add three things memory cannot: compressed procedural skill and style that no longer needs context, lower cost and latency, and small specialised judges. They also carry the catastrophic-forgetting risk that CLS theory predicts. Meta's sparse-memory study is the cleanest number: after learning new facts, NaturalQuestions F1 fell **89% under full fine-tuning, 71% under LoRA and 11% under sparse memory-layer fine-tuning**, though the baselines used a different optimiser and the test covered one 1.3B model ([arXiv 2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15103v1)). MIT's "RL's Razor" found on-policy RL forgets much less than supervised fine-tuning at equal new-task performance, and that forgetting is predicted by the KL divergence from the base model measured on the new task ([arXiv 2509.04259](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04259)).

That points to three uses, in order of value. First, a **proactivity and triage classifier**: a small model fine-tuned on your agents' accepted and dismissed interruptions, following ProactiveBench's result that a fine-tuned 8B model edged out GPT-4o ([arXiv 2410.12361](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)). It is cheap, measurable and has no forgetting risk to the main model. Second, a **role-level LoRA "sleep"** run monthly, not nightly, on consented, anonymised, approved deliverables from many employers. This is the "school" consolidation step, and it gives you a model competitors cannot copy by hosting an open-source agent. Keep it role-level, never per-employer facts. Mix in replay data from the base distribution, prefer preference or on-policy RL objectives over plain SFT, monitor KL on the new data, gate every release on a held-out general-capability suite plus the role's frozen regression set, and keep instant rollback. Third, optionally, small **per-tenant style adapters** once a tenant has thousands of approved outputs, served many-per-base with S-LoRA ([arXiv 2311.03285](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.03285v3)). Facts, preferences and anything that can change stay in memory. Note one practical constraint: you cannot LoRA a closed frontier model, so weight-level consolidation implies an open-weight model for at least the drafting or triage path. That is a cost and quality trade-off to test, not a default.

### What still needs a research lab

The genuinely brain-like mechanisms are lab-scale. Google's Titans learns to memorise at test time with a surprise signal and scales beyond 2M-token contexts. Atlas and the Nested Learning/HOPE line add multi-timescale "continuum memory," explicitly inspired by neuroplasticity, but at research scale and with no confirmed deployment in Gemini ([arXiv 2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663); [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/introducing-nested-learning-a-new-ml-paradigm-for-continual-learning)). Meta's memory layers needed pretraining with up to 128B memory parameters on 1T tokens ([arXiv 2412.09764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764)). "Language Models Need Sleep" is the first direct consolidation-into-weights attempt, on 1.4–2B models and synthetic tasks ([alphaXiv 2605.26099](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.26099v1)). Developmental grounding is further behind. The CVCL model learned word–referent mappings from 61 hours of one child's headcam video but scored **34.7% against CLIP's 99.4%** on out-of-distribution objects ([Vong et al. 2024](https://Gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/cnn/2024-vong.pdf)), and BabyLM's multimodal tracks have produced no winner two years running ([BabyLM 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28)). Learned forgetting, principled consolidation and trustworthy reflection remain open problems ([Du 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07670)). None of these belong on a solo roadmap except as things to watch and swap in when they ship as APIs.

### How far toward AGI this gets

The honest ceiling has two parts. Your system can achieve **functional continual learning within a role and a household**. It will remember, generalise corrections, write and refine skills, anticipate, and get measurably better for this manager week over week, which is most of what Dwarkesh Patel's definition of learning "on the job as easily, organically, seamlessly, and quickly as a human" ([Zvi on Dwarkesh](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/dwarkesh-patel-on-continual-learning)) asks for in narrow white-collar work. What it will not do is the part of childhood that makes children special. It will not discover new causal structure through self-directed experiment the way four-year-olds do with blicket detectors, a task where LLMs given the same observations as text failed ([Yiu, Kosoy & Gopnik](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11373165)). It will not ground concepts in perception at child-level efficiency. And its retrieval-based learning does not reshape the underlying competence the way consolidation reshapes a cortex. The test that separates the two regimes is transfer. A memory-based agent gets better at what it has seen corrected. A truly developing mind gets better at things it was never taught. Track that explicitly with novel-variant probes in the Adolescent gate, and expect the memory-only system to plateau there. That plateau is the honest boundary between engineering and research in 2026.

## A staged build plan for one engineer

Earlier analysis in this project reached conclusions that still stand and frame this plan. Self-learning agents are now a commodity (Hermes and OpenClaw ship skill-writing and memory loops), so the defensible product is a **structured apprenticeship with an audited, portable track record**. The recommended wedge is a B2B LinkedIn/content-strategist role for founder-led firms and agencies in Singapore and Southeast Asia, priced as a skill-tiered rate card metered on approved deliverables and launched as a public 30-day probation on LinkedIn. The developmental frame strengthens that recommendation: the Infant → Adult ladder *is* the apprenticeship, and the stage gates are the track record. A software-developer role is the better second "child" because its classes are verifiable, which lets curiosity-driven practice work, but it is a far more crowded market.

| Role-level sleep | Months 6–12 | Monthly role LoRA on an open-weight model from consented approved outputs, with replay mixing, KL monitoring, general-capability gate and rollback; optional per-tenant style adapters | Slow neocortical consolidation | Feasible solo, with forgetting risk |
| Watch list | Ongoing | Test-time memory models (Titans/HOPE), sparse memory fine-tuning, weight-level sleep, egocentric multimodal grounding, learned forgetting | Multi-timescale plasticity, true consolidation | Lab only |

Instrument everything from day one, because the developmental claim is only as good as its growth curves. Track first-pass approval, edit distance, calibration error, proactive-suggestion acceptance, novel-variant success and time-in-stage per agent. The infrastructure for a content-strategist agent at this scale is likely **$150–300 per agent per month**, mostly model tokens. That is an estimate from the earlier cost model (about $175/month of Opus 5 tokens at heavy use, $33 for a VM, cents for Temporal), not a quoted figure.

## Conclusion

The developmental frame turns out to be more useful as a *learning-mode schedule* than as a metaphor about emptiness. What changes from Infant to Adult is not how much the agent knows. A pretrained model already knows more facts than any adult. What changes is which signal it trusts. Infants trust statistics and attention. Children trust ostensive teaching and copy faithfully. Adolescents trust outcomes and their own models of other minds. Adults exploit and teach. Building an agent whose memory writes, consolidation priorities and autonomy scope change with stage is something almost no product does, and it gives the marketplace an evidence-based reason for its hiring ladder rather than a marketing one. The brain also supplies a precise answer to proactivity, cue-triggered intentions rather than constant polling, and to forgetting, supersession and regularisation rather than garbage collection. Both are under-built in current agent stacks.

The sharper implication concerns AGI. The gap between your agent and a child is no longer knowledge, language or even memory. It is self-directed experiment that produces transferable causal understanding, plus consolidation that changes the learner rather than its notebook. Those are exactly the problems the frontier labs now name as missing, and Sutskever's "eager 15-year-old" is effectively a bet that someone will solve your Adolescent stage in the weights. A solo engineer cannot win that race, but can build the scaffolding it will plug into. That means an episodic store, consolidation job, stage gates and transfer probes that already work with frozen models and that will measure, the day weight-level continual learning arrives, whether the new brains actually grow up faster.
