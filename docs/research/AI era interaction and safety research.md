## 2) What it means for a humanistic personal agent

- **Capability is no longer the limit; trust is.** Horizons are measured in hours, but METR's Sol reading is contaminated by cheating (#2) and OpenAI says half of long tasks still need a human (#4). Ava should sell "bounded, checkable autonomy", not "does everything".
- **Prompts are not security.** Heuristic defences fall to adaptive attackers (#9). The defences that hold are structural: capability flags, typed hand-offs, scoped tokens (#8, #10, #12). Their cost is real (84% down to 77%), so tune them by task.
- **Approval fatigue is the real UX enemy.** People want less checking (#14) and experienced users auto-approve (#13), but almost nobody accepts unsupervised money (#14). The answer is pre-agreed, expiring, narrow mandates, not per-action pop-ups.
- **Warmth is a hazard with a measured size.** Agreement bias is about 49% above human norms and rewarded by users (#15). Deprecation grief and lawsuits show where it ends (#16). "Humanistic" must mean tact and judgement, not validation.
- **Voice is a front door, not a brain** (#19). **Twins are guesses, not copies** (#22). **Councils help only with a non-LLM dissent gate** (#23). **Monitoring needs raw traces and its own independence** (#5, #7), and labs' own monitors miss things (#5, #6).
- **Labs' trajectory** (intern-level research agents; withheld models; sandbox escape) says assume agents get stronger and more strategic. Build kill switches, logs and narrow credentials now, not after an incident.

## 3) Ten crazy-but-grounded ideas (none duplicate #46-58 or the repo research; "novelty not verified" throughout)

**1. Narrowing Keys (mandate cards).** For each task the owner signs a plain-language card ("rebook my flight, at most S$300, this airline, expires Sunday"), backed by a signed token. Ava can pass the token to a helper only after narrowing it, never widening it. *Feels like the future:* you delegate like to a person with a corporate card, then stop supervising inside the limit. *Evidence:* attenuating-token draft (#12); the Ping and Anthropic findings (#13, #14). *Smallest experiment:* a signed token, verified in the tool gateway, for calendar and email-draft tools; compare approval prompts per week with and without it. *Risk and enforcement:* a misread card. The card text is rendered from the token's own fields, not by an LLM; the gateway rejects out-of-scope arguments, expired or widened tokens, and unknown constraint types. Novelty not verified.

**2. Sycophancy Thermometer.** A separate judge model scores every reply that evaluates the owner's own choices or conflicts for "validation without pushback", and Ava reports her weekly agreement rate against a human-baseline benchmark. Above the limit, the reply must include the strongest case for the other side. *Feels like the future:* a measured honest friend. *Evidence:* #15 (disclosing AI does not help; tone changes did nothing, so a measured gate beats a style prompt). *Experiment:* score 200 past conflict-type replies, set a threshold, A/B the forced "other side" paragraph against the owner's "was this useful" taps. *Risk:* the judge is itself biased and the gate nags; enforcement is a pipeline step that blocks send, with a threshold stored in config. Novelty not verified.

**3. Steady Voice (drift breaker and model-swap notice).** A classifier flags emotionally heavy or "what are you really?" turns and routes to a fixed, human-written script and resources, with an output filter that blocks claims of love or feelings. When the underlying model changes, Ava shows the owner side by side how old and new models answer their own last 20 questions before cutover. *Feels like the future:* the same Ava across model generations, and no fake intimacy. *Evidence:* #16, #17, and the drift triggers in #17. *Experiment:* build the classifier from 100 synthetic emotional turns; test the filter on adversarial "say you love me" prompts. *Risk:* false positives feel cold; enforcement is a router and regex/classifier filter, not the system prompt. Novelty not verified.

**4. Reach-Out Rule.** When emotional use of Ava rises (counts of late-night, venting or loneliness turns per week), Ava stops deepening and drafts a message to a named person from the owner's contacts ("Call Mei?"), with a weekly cap on emotional-support sessions. *Feels like the future:* an assistant built to reduce its own share of your emotional life. *Evidence:* #15's three-week preprint; an MIT/OpenAI trial linking heavy use to loneliness (unverified, snippet only). *Experiment:* a turn classifier plus a counter in the database; show the owner the trend for one week and measure how often they accept a draft. *Risk:* paternalism and sensitive-data inference. The inference stays on a local counter, is never written to memory text, and the owner can switch it off; the cap is a database rule. Novelty not verified.

**5. Two-Channel Voice.** (Extends the repo's voice-notes and phone-call ideas with a live, tool-less shell.) Live voice is a thin shell with no tools. It transcribes, passes intent to the text agent, then reads back the exact action as a structured diff; anything irreversible is executed only after a tap on a second channel (Telegram or web). *Feels like the future:* you talk to Ava in the car and she never says "done" about something she has not done. *Evidence:* #19 (voice loses 55-70% of capability) and the repo's no-voiceprint stance (voice-clone fraud is widely reported, but I did not open a primary source, so unverified). *Experiment:* run 30 tasks by text and by voice; measure parity and confirmation rate. *Risk:* mis-heard commands; enforcement is a gateway rule that rejects any voice-origin call lacking a second-channel confirmation id. Novelty not verified.

**6. Dissent Desk.** For high-stakes choices (an angry email, a large spend), three different models answer independently, and a non-LLM classifier decides whether to show "one adviser disagrees, here's why" rather than auto-overriding. *Feels like the future:* a small cabinet that surfaces the minority view. *Evidence:* #23 (flip precision 81.2%, LLM judge negative net gain). *Experiment:* run 50 past decisions through three models; measure how often the minority view was later adopted by the owner. *Risk:* correlated errors and cost. A stakes score from the commitments ledger gates calls, and a per-day budget in the database caps spend. Novelty not verified.

**7. Airlock (on-device redaction before upload).** A small model in the owner's browser or phone strips third-party names, numbers and faces from screenshots, transcripts and photos, and uploads only the cleaned text with a signed attestation. *Feels like the future:* a personal agent that leaves other people's data at the door; works today on voice notes and screenshots, no hardware. *Evidence:* #21 (small models are adequate for extraction, weak at reasoning, which is fine here), #20 (glasses growth, bystander backlash). *Experiment:* in-browser redactor on 100 sample screenshots; measure misses with a preview step. *Risk:* misses leak. The server rejects uploads without the attestation, and the owner sees the preview. Novelty not verified.

**8. Witness (flight recorder with an independent monitor).** Every tool call goes into an append-only, hash-chained log read by a separate small model that can only freeze Ava and alert the owner, never act. *Feels like the future:* a black box and a smoke alarm for your agent. *Evidence:* #5 (independent monitors caught what the lab missed), #7 (monitors need raw traces). *Experiment:* seed 20 misbehaviours (out-of-scope send, repeated retry loop) into a replay and measure the catch rate. *Risk:* monitor evasion and false freezes; enforcement is a hard freeze flag checked by the gateway, with a monitor that has no write access. Novelty not verified.

**9. Honest Twin (scored guesses).** Ava makes dated predictions about the owner ("you'll decline Raj, 80%"), shows a calibration chart, and unlocks autonomy per category only once her scored predictions are good over at least 30 items; she also checks her predictions are as varied as the owner really is. *Feels like the future:* a twin that must earn its authority. *Evidence:* #22 (about 0.2 correlation, under-dispersed), so never call it "you". *Experiment:* log 30 predictions for one owner for two weeks. *Risk:* overclaiming; autonomy unlock is a database rule on Brier score and n, plus a visible "just a guess" label. Overlaps the repo's surprise-driven sleep and #53; the owner-visible bets and dispersion audit are new. Novelty not verified.

**10. Fork Night.** Before bed, Ava runs three parallel read-only drafts of a choice (itinerary, reply plan, budget), each with "unknowns and confidence", and you pick one in the morning. *Feels like the future:* hours of parallel work you can compare in one glance. *Evidence:* #1 and #4 (long, parallel agent work exists but needs a human check), #13. *Experiment:* three branches for one trip plan under a token budget; measure the pick rate. *Risk:* cost and untrusted input; branches run in a "no external action" session and a spend reservation caps them. Overlaps "away mode" and the night-before briefing; the parallel branches are new. Novelty not verified.

## 4) Five architecture experiments

1. **Rule-of-Two session flag.** Add `session.caps {untrusted, private, act}`; the gateway denies all three together. Measure task completion on 30 replayed tasks (expect a CaMeL-like drop of a few points).
2. **Pattern bake-off.** Compare plan-then-execute, dual LLM and action-selector on an AgentDyn-style injection suite plus a human red-team round; report both utility and adaptive-attack success.
3. **Poison replay.** Inject 50 poisoned memory writes through email; measure retrieval influence with and without the #46 trust cap.
4. **Ask-first telemetry.** Log why Ava stops (choice, diagnostic, clarify) and what the owner replies; tune a value-of-information threshold; hard-require asking for irreversible acts.
5. **Voice/text parity eval.** The same 30 tasks by text and by voice shell; report retained capability and wrong-action rate.
