# Rewire Ava to remember like a chief of staff

To feel like a real human assistant, Ava needs three things a good executive assistant has and Ava lacks: **a memory that learns from every conversation and sleeps on it, a model of each person in the owner's life that includes what that person already knows, and a ledger of promises that comes back at the right moment**. Today she has none of the three. Ava's identity and audience gate is genuinely strong. Behind it, though, memory is a flat Postgres list that hands the model the newest 30 rows whatever was asked ([audience.py:80](../../backend/src/superaide/context/audience.py#L80)). Ordinary chat writes nothing. No background job exists, so nothing consolidates, forgets, reminds or follows up. The memory science and the 2024–2026 open-source code point the same way. Keep raw episodes as ground truth. Derive bi-temporal facts from them, with provenance. Retrieve with a hybrid score inside the existing Postgres, behind the existing SQL privacy gate. Run a nightly "sleep" job that segments, replays by priority, reconciles and promotes into an owner-readable wiki. **The notebook should not be a vector database.** It should be a curated, cited, owner-editable wiki, with pgvector and full-text search as a derived index beside it, which is what ADR 0002 already anticipates. Access to the owner's world (calendar first, then email metadata, then drafts) should open one OAuth scope at a time as Ava climbs from Infant to Adult. Two fixes come first, because every integration depends on them: the approval queue can never be decided or executed today, and the policy engine does not enforce `min_trust`. At Haiku 5.5 prices the whole memory pipeline costs on the order of a dollar a month. The chat model, not memory, is what threatens the spend cap.

## Human assistants earn access in five rungs, and Ava can realistically reach about half of the owner's world

### What the job actually is

The real job is dominated by two surfaces. In a 2025 survey of 600 executive assistants, the main time sink was **scheduling and calendar management for 29.3%, and email and communications for 21.5%**. Projects (16.3%), travel booking (13.5%) and meeting prep (7.2%) followed, and more than half of EAs support two or more principals ([Vimcal EA 2026 Report](https://www.vimcal.com/ea/2026-report)). The sample is self-selected and senior, but the ranking matches everything else in the notes. Calendar and inbox are where an assistant earns its keep. They are also where the most untrusted inbound content arrives.

### How trust is graduated

Trust is graduated on two axes at once: what the assistant may do, and how much of the owner's world it may see. Michael Hyatt's widely used five levels of delegation run from "do exactly what I say" through "research and report", "recommend", and "decide and tell me afterward", to "run with it" ([Portfolink summary](https://portfolink.com/blog/five-levels-delegation/)). Mail systems encode the same ladder. Exchange delegates move from Reviewer (read) to Author (create, with Send on Behalf) to Editor (change the owner's items) ([Drexel KB](https://www.lebow.drexel.edu/resources/technology/knowledge-base/allowing-colleague-manage-your-email-and-calendar)). Gmail delegation, by contrast, is all-or-nothing on the mailbox ([SupportBee](https://supportbee.com/blog/how-to-delegate-gmail-access)). Delegation guides recommend a separate scope axis: triage without reply rights, then drafts for approval, then sending, and thread-specific or filtered access before full-mailbox access ([Magic](https://getmagic.com/guides/how-to-delegate-your-email/); [Athena](https://www.athena.com/blog/posts/delegate-emails)).

Ava's Infant→Adult stages map onto this ladder almost one to one. Infant is Hyatt L1–L2: observe, read and report. Child is L3: draft everything, and the owner approves each action. Adolescent is L4: act within a written policy, with undo, and report in the digest. Adult is L5: standing authority for whitelisted action classes, escalating by exception. The useful design move is to make **each rung a narrower OAuth scope set**, not just a prompt paragraph. No source documents how human EAs learn the unwritten rules: who always gets through, which meetings are sacred, gift budgets. The AI analogue has to be explicit. It means owner-editable preference memory plus learning from corrections to drafts. That is procedural memory, covered below.

Social, finance, health and home APIs are closed or hostile, and Ava should reach them only through what the owner forwards, uploads or receives by email.

### The security model has to change before any of this is connected

An inbox-connected assistant has Simon Willison's **"lethal trifecta" by construction**: private data, untrusted content, and a way to communicate out. Willison calls guardrails that stop 95% of attacks "a failing grade" ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711, **CVSS 9.3**) showed a zero-click email exfiltrating data from Microsoft 365 Copilot. It slipped past the injection classifier and leaked through reference-style Markdown links and auto-fetched images ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/zero-click-ai-vulnerability-exposes.html)). The open-source cautionary tale is OpenClaw. It combined about 22 inbound channels, host execution defaulting to "full" with approvals off, and prompt-injection defence limited to labelling. In 2026 that produced **135,000+ exposed instances** ([The Register](https://theregister.com/2026/02/09/openclaw_instances_exposed_vibe_code)), a 1-click RCE rated CVSS 8.8 ([GitHub advisory](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/security/advisories/GHSA-g8p2-7wf7-98mq)), and **341 malicious skills out of 2,857** on its registry ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/researchers-find-341-malicious-clawhub.html)).

The defences that work are architectural. Untrusted content (email bodies, invites, web pages) is read by a tool-less "reader" call that returns typed fields and can never trigger egress. Rendered output never auto-loads images or external links. Every outbound message, booking or post goes through an approval with an append-only audit row. Scopes are the minimum for the current stage. Refresh tokens stay in the existing AES-GCM vault and never enter model context. For remote tool servers, the MCP authorization spec already mandates PKCE, audience-bound tokens and no token passthrough ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/basic/authorization)).

Ava is architecturally ahead of OpenClaw here. Strangers get no model call, identity is decided in code, and the model cannot write memory ([pipeline.py:1](../../backend/src/superaide/pipeline.py#L1)). But the approval mechanism is only half built. `decide` always queues an approval and never compares the requester's trust with `rule.min_trust` ([actions.py:44-71](../../backend/src/superaide/policy/actions.py#L44)). No endpoint approves, and no executor acts. **Draft-for-approval, the Child stage, is impossible until those exist.**

On the legal side, India's DPDP core obligations (notice, consent, security, retention) take effect on **13 May 2027** ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Personal-use exemptions in PDPA, DPDP and GDPR may cover the owner. They are unlikely to cover the operator who processes correspondents' data, and that is unverified. Retention limits on third-party raw messages are therefore a design requirement, not a nicety.

## Memory science prescribes separate stores, salience at write time, and forgetting as demotion

Human memory is not one store with a search box. It is several systems that store different things and change at different rates, and almost every system maps onto a specific Ava defect. This section extracts the design rules. The table at the end maps each finding to a mechanism.

### Separate systems, a tiny working buffer, and intentions as cue → action rules

Tulving's split between **episodic** memory (dated, situated events) and **semantic** memory (context-free knowledge) is the foundation ([Tulving 1985](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0080017)). Patient H.M. kept learning mirror-drawing with no new declarative memory, which separated **procedural** skill from both ([Squire 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2004.06.005)). **Prospective** memory, remembering to do something later, works best when it is event-cued rather than time-cued. Time cues need effortful clock-watching ([McDaniel & Einstein 2000](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/strategic-and-automatic-processes-in-prospective-memory-retrieval/)). Implementation intentions ("if X, then I will Y") hand cue detection to the environment and improve goal attainment with an effect of **d = 0.65** across 94 tests ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://stafforini.com/works/gollwitzer-2006-implementation-intentions-and/)). Working memory holds **about 4 chunks** ([Cowan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922)). Experts exceed that only through fast retrieval structures in long-term memory ([Ericsson & Kintsch 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.2.211)), like the waiter who held 20 dinner orders by encoding them by course and seat ([Ericsson & Polson 1988](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.14.2.305)).

For Ava, the rule is plain. Episodes, facts, procedures, intentions and people need separate tables with separate write and decay policies. The prompt should carry a small, structured "focus" (the current goal, the counterpart, open commitments, the key constraint), backed by retrieval, rather than today's 30 undifferentiated rows.

### Salience, novelty and event boundaries decide what is worth keeping

Emotional arousal strengthens consolidation through the amygdala. Propranolol abolished the memory advantage of an emotional story but left a neutral one untouched ([Cahill et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1038/371702a0)). Emotion inflates *confidence* far more than *accuracy*, though: flashbulb memories of 9/11 stayed vivid while their consistency decayed like ordinary memories ([Talarico & Rubin 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.02453)). Novelty and prediction error drive the hippocampal–dopamine loop that makes memories last ([Lisman & Grace 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.05.002)). A weak memory can be rescued by a strong event that follows it within hours ([Frey & Morris 1997](https://doi.org/10.1038/385533a0); [Dunsmoor et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14106)). Information that fits an existing schema integrates quickly. Schema-violating information is stored as a distinct episode ([van Kesteren et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2012.02.001)). People also segment experience into events at moments of prediction error, and items within one event are bound more tightly than items across a boundary ([Kurby & Zacks 2008](https://www.gvsu.edu/cms4/asset/92386C7F-BF75-96ED-67D9FAA81701D122/kurby__zacks_2008.pdf); [DuBrow & Davachi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0033431)). Event boundaries also make pending intentions less accessible: the "doorway effect" ([Radvansky & Zacks 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5734104)).

Four design rules follow. **Importance is scored at write time** from self-relevance, affect, surprise, goal relevance, social commitment and an explicit "remember" flag. **Importance and confidence are stored as separate fields**, so an emotional memory gets retrieval priority but no extra evidential weight. **The log is segmented into episodes at boundaries** (a time gap, a change of participants, channel or topic, or an affect spike) rather than at fixed windows. And **open intentions are re-surfaced explicitly at every boundary**, because that is exactly when people drop them.

### Consolidation is selective replay, biased toward what the future needs

Complementary Learning Systems theory is the most transferable blueprint. A fast hippocampal learner stores episodes in one shot. A slow neocortical learner extracts structure. Interleaved replay lets the slow learner absorb new material without catastrophically overwriting the old ([McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)). Replay is goal-weighted, not frequency-weighted ([Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcClelland16FinalMS.pdf)). The normative account says to replay in proportion to **gain × need**: how much replaying an item would improve behaviour, times how likely it is to be needed ([Mattar & Daw 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0232-z)). Sleep preferentially consolidates what people *expect to need*. Participants told about a later test benefited from sleep, and uninformed ones did not ([Wilhelm et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3575-10.2011)). With a schema in place, consolidation can finish within about 48 hours rather than weeks ([Tse et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2271071)), although that timescale is contested ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2022.923713/pdf)). Over time verbatim detail fades and gist survives ([Reyna & Brainerd 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/1041-6080(95)90031-4)). Trace-transformation theory holds that vivid detail always depends on the original hippocampal trace ([Winocur & Moscovitch 2011](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1355617711000683)). In Ava's terms, that trace is the raw `event` log.

The engineering evidence agrees with the neuroscience on caution. In the repo's earlier landscape research, Microsoft's human-inspired consolidation never beat raw retrieval on LongMemEval (**76.8 vs 78.4**), and aggressive consolidation **collapsed to 48.4** ([Brain inspired memory landscape and identity.md:57-65](Brain%20inspired%20memory%20landscape%20and%20identity.md#L57)). The sleep job must therefore *add* derived structure and *never delete* raw episodes.

### Recall is the moment to update, but updates must be versioned

Reactivating a consolidated memory can make it labile and rewritable ([Nader, Schafe & LeDoux 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35021052)). In humans the boundary conditions are shaky. A preregistered attempt failed to replicate the prediction-error trigger ([Stemerding et al. 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8831535/)). Human memory also overwrites itself without an audit trail, which is the misinformation effect ([Loftus 2005](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.94705)). Ava should keep the sound core and do better than biology.

**Retrieval plus mismatch** is the update signal. A small mismatch refines a fact in place. A correction supersedes the old fact and keeps its validity window. A regime change (a new job, a divorce) closes the old fact and opens a new one. Owner corrections outrank everything, and dependent summaries are re-opened when a fact they rest on changes.

### Forgetting is adaptive, and it should mean demotion, not deletion

Forgetting follows a power function, not an exponential ([Wixted & Ebbesen 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1991.tb00175.x)). The probability that an item will be needed again follows the same recency and frequency laws in newspaper headlines, parental speech and email ([Anderson & Schooler 1991](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.1991.tb00174.x/)). In other words, memory forgets what the environment stopped asking for. Forgetting earns its keep by suppressing outdated information and preventing overfitting ([Richards & Frankland 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.04.037)). Practising some items suppresses their unpractised neighbours, which is retrieval-induced forgetting ([Anderson, Bjork & Bjork 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.20.5.1063)). An assistant that keeps surfacing the same three friends could therefore crowd the rest out of the owner's mind.

Spacing has a quantified sweet spot. The best re-study gap is about **20–40% of a one-week horizon and 5–10% of a one-year horizon** ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/)). That gives Ava a principled schedule for re-verifying uncertain facts. A fact that must hold for a year gets re-checked about monthly; one needed next week gets re-checked within a day or two.

### Retrieval is cue overlap, and ACT-R already gives a usable scoring function

A cue works only if it was encoded with the target ([Tulving & Thomson 1973](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0020071)). Recalling one item cues its temporal neighbours, with a forward bias ([Howard & Kahana 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmps.2001.1388)). ACT-R turns this into arithmetic ([Anderson et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.1036)). **Base-level activation** is B = ln Σ t_k^(−d), summed over each past use, with a conventional decay of d = 0.5 ([ACT-R primer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18055)). **Associative strength** falls with a cue's fan, S_ji ≈ S − ln(fan_j), so a cue linked to hundreds of memories (the owner) spreads little activation while a rare one ("Dr. Mehta") spreads a lot. Retrieval itself is a logistic gate on activation minus a threshold. This is the backbone of Ava's scoring formula below. Recency and frequency become one principled term instead of a hand-tuned half-life.

### Other minds, promises and shared memory

Children pass false-belief tasks at about age 4–6. Modelling what *someone else* wrongly believes is the core of theory of mind ([Wimmer & Perner 1983](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(83)90004-5)). Humans tag sources poorly and misremember who said what ([Johnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay 1993](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3)). They keep relationships in layers of roughly 5, 15, 50 and 150 ([Zhou et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2004.2970)). Socially important intentions are remembered better than neutral ones ([Brandimonte et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/09541440903180085)). Couples share memory work implicitly. When an experimenter imposed a division of labour, natural couples did *worse* than strangers ([Wegner, Erber & Raymond 1991](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/dwegner/files/wegnererberraymond1991.pdf)).

The external-memory tradition adds a final lesson. Total-capture lifelogs go unused. What helps are tools aimed at the "five Rs": recollecting, reminiscing, retrieving, reflecting and remembering intentions ([Sellen & Whittaker 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1735223.1735243)). Associative trails, not storage, are what made the memex compelling ([Bush 1945](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/)).

| Finding | Design rule for Ava |
|---|---|
| Separate memory systems (Tulving, Squire) | Separate tables and policies for episodes, facts, procedures, intentions, people |
| ~4-chunk working memory (Cowan) | A structured four-slot "focus" in the prompt, plus a small supporting set |
| Emotion raises confidence, not accuracy (Talarico & Rubin) | `importance` and `confidence` are separate columns; emotion touches only the first |
| Event segmentation, doorway effect (Zacks, Radvansky) | Segment episodes at boundaries; re-surface open intentions at each boundary |
| CLS replay, gain × need, sleep favours expected use | A nightly job replays the top-priority episodes, biased by tomorrow's calendar and open intentions |
| Aggressive consolidation hurts (Microsoft, via prior report) | Never delete raw episodes; derived layers only add |
| Reconsolidation on mismatch; misinformation effect | Update on retrieval plus mismatch; supersede with history; owner corrections win |
| Power-law forgetting, rational analysis | ACT-R activation drives demotion; hard delete only for privacy or the owner's request |
| Spacing ratio (Cepeda) | Re-verify uncertain facts at roughly 5–40% of their needed horizon |
| False belief, source monitoring | Per-person knowledge state: who was told what, by whom, when |
| Prosocial PM, implementation intentions | A commitments ledger with event-based triggers on the counterparty |
| Transactive memory, five Rs | Negotiate a division of labour; offer a "help me remember" mode |

## The 2024–2026 systems converge on Postgres, raw episodes and bi-temporal facts, and their code differs from their papers

### Four families, and benchmark claims that cannot be ranked

Agent memory now comes in four families: **agent-paged context** (MemGPT/Letta, MIRIX), **LLM-extracted fact stores with reconciliation** (Mem0, LangMem, Memory-R1), **knowledge graphs** (Graphiti/Zep, Cognee, HippoRAG, GraphRAG) and **LLM-maintained documents** (Karpathy's LLM Wiki, Letta's filesystem agent, Claude's file-based memory tool). The most important empirical fact about them is uncomfortable. **Simple baselines keep winning on recall.** In Mem0's own LoCoMo table, full context scored **72.90** against Mem0's 66.88 and Mem0g's 68.44. Mem0's real advantage was latency and about 90% fewer tokens, not accuracy ([Mem0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413v1)). A Letta agent with nothing but grep and file tools over the raw conversation scored **74.0%** ([Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory)). Zep re-ran its own system after alleging misconfiguration and reported **75.14%** ([Zep](https://www.getzep.com/blog/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)). Vendor LoCoMo claims range from the 50s to the 90s under different judges, prompts and excluded categories. The same MIRIX system is reported at 85.4% by its authors and 64.33% by MemOS's ([MemOS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03724v4)). They are not comparable.

LongMemEval (ICLR 2025) is the more discriminating test. It has 500 questions over histories of about 115k tokens and tests extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention. Commercial assistants drop about 30% on it ([LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)). Even there the trade-off shows. With gpt-4o, Zep beat full context overall (**71.2 vs 60.2**), but recall of what the *assistant* had said fell from **94.6 to 80.4**, because extraction abstracts assistant turns away ([Zep paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1)). MemoryAgentBench finds that no architecture masters accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding and conflict resolution together ([MemoryAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05257)).

The lesson for Ava is that **derived memory buys latency, tokens, cross-session synthesis and update handling, while the raw log buys recall**. She needs both, and her own evaluation must always include a full-context baseline and a grep baseline.

### What the code actually does

Reading the repositories at their October 2026 commits changes several assumptions a reader would take from the papers.

**Mem0** is the most-starred project (67.6k), and its open-source library no longer does what its paper describes. Writes are a single **ADD-only** LLM call. Contradictions are linked, not resolved. Graph memory has moved to the paid platform. Dedup is an exact MD5 match against 10 neighbours. Scoring has no recency term, and its keyword sigmoid is likely miscalibrated against `ts_rank_cd`. Telemetry to PostHog is on by default (`docs/migration/oss-v2-to-v3.mdx:14-19`; `mem0/utils/scoring.py:31-56`; `mem0/memory/telemetry.py:14-16` at [Mem0 commit 2c81af3](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0/blob/2c81af3bbe27d9bccb09c772d583df9e9be72ee5/)). For a long-lived personal assistant, ADD-only memory without validity grows monotonically and serves conflicting facts with equal weight. It is a pattern to avoid.

**Graphiti** is the best-engineered system, and its key idea is a division of labour. The LLM only judges, from text, whether facts are duplicates or contradictions. Deterministic code applies the bi-temporal algebra: it sets the old edge's `invalid_at` to the new edge's `valid_at`, and marks a late-arriving older fact as "born superseded" (`graphiti_core/utils/maintenance/edge_operations.py:715-783` at [Graphiti commit 8e320f8](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti/blob/8e320f880e7d169a907dbf2a7ac4188586c03b55/)). Entity resolution is tiered: exact name, then an entropy gate, then MinHash Jaccard ≥ 0.9, then one batched LLM call for the leftovers. That saves most LLM calls. Its defaults should not be copied. RRF uses k = 1 rather than 60. Searches return invalidated facts unless filtered. And `search()` mutates a module-global recipe that ingestion also uses (`graphiti.py:1955-1958`).

**Letta's** Python server now lives on an `archive` branch, and `main` holds only a README ([Letta](https://github.com/letta-ai/letta)). Its core-memory **blocks** are still worth stealing. They are labelled and described, rendered with `chars_current/limit`, and edited by a unique-match `memory_replace` that fails on zero or multiple matches. But the code never enforces the limit, and concurrent edits are last-writer-wins.

**A-MEM's** public library never calls its note-enrichment function, its LLM links never resolve to real ids, and `update_neighbor` edits the wrong notes (`agentic_memory/memory_system.py:159`, `:684-716` at [A-MEM commit ceffb86](https://github.com/agiresearch/A-mem/blob/ceffb860f0712bbae97b184d440df62bc910ca8d/)). Take the concepts, not the code. Its bugs are exactly what Mem0's old "show the LLM short integer ids and map them back" trick prevents.

**HippoRAG 2** matches its paper: facts are filtered by an LLM, then Personalized PageRank spreads over an entity-plus-passage graph. It reports a **7% associative-memory gain** over a strong embedder ([HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)). But graph construction for it, A-Mem and RAPTOR on LongMemEval-M took over a week ([arXiv 2610.11573](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11573)). **MIRIX's** typed Postgres tables (core, episodic with `occurred_at` and `actor`, semantic, procedural, resource, and a sensitive "knowledge vault") are the closest existing analogue to a Postgres-native personal-assistant schema. Note that its "bm25" is really `ts_rank_cd`.

**OpenClaw** carries the ideas that suit Ava best, despite its security record. Human-readable Markdown (`MEMORY.md`, `USER.md`) is the source of truth, injected under character caps and **only in private sessions**. A derived SQLite index fuses FTS5 and vectors at 0.7/0.3, applies a 30-day decay only to dated notes, and uses MMR. Every memory write carries an origin class (owner, agent, untrusted, system). **Content written during a turn tainted by untrusted input can never be auto-injected or promoted** (`src/agents/agent-tools.ts:198-216`; `extensions/memory-core/src/short-term-promotion.ts:104` at [OpenClaw commit be30004](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/blob/be300040596d9feb0bdf34939a889ac81d54b0ac/)).

Its nightly "dreaming" job promotes a snippet into curated memory only after it has been recalled at least 3 times by at least 3 distinct queries within 30 days. The score is weighted **0.24 frequency, 0.30 relevance, 0.15 diversity, 0.15 recency, 0.10 consolidation and 0.06 conceptual**. Every promoted line must cite its source, and an update may remove at most 25% of prior content (`short-term-promotion.ts:30-37`). Karpathy's LLM Wiki is the same pattern in the open. Immutable raw sources sit under an LLM-written, cross-linked wiki with an index, an append-only log and a periodic "lint" for contradictions and stale claims. It works at "~100 sources, hundreds of pages" with no embedding RAG ([Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)).

### Storage verdict: one Postgres, no separate vector database, no graph database

At one owner's scale, **pgvector plus native full-text search inside Ava's existing Postgres is sufficient and strictly better** than adding Neo4j or a vector database. pgvector 0.8.0 added iterative index scans, which fix "overfiltering" when a WHERE clause (such as Ava's audience predicate) leaves fewer than k results after an approximate scan ([PostgreSQL.org](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/pgvector-080-released-2952/)). `halfvec` halves storage. HNSW indexes `vector` up to 2,000 dimensions and `halfvec` up to 4,000 ([pgvector README](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector#indexing)), which rules out Letta's and MIRIX's 4096-padding. The repo's own estimate puts 10,000 facts at 1024-dim `halfvec` at about **20 MB**, small enough for an exact scan ([Personal assistant memory architecture.md:65-67](Personal%20assistant%20memory%20architecture.md#L65)). The graph work a personal assistant needs is one or two hops from a person, project or place. An `entity`/`edge` table pair with a recursive CTE handles it, and Apache AGE remains an option for openCypher later ([AGE](https://github.com/apache/age)).

Keeping everything in one store has a decisive advantage for Ava in particular. **The audience gate, similarity search, validity filter and erase cascade stay in one SQL statement and one transaction**, which is exactly why ADR 0002 chose Postgres ([0002-postgres-as-the-only-store.md:11-17](../../docs/decisions/0002-postgres-as-the-only-store.md#L11)). A separate vector store would force the privacy filter to run after the fact. That is the post-filter-after-LIMIT failure the teardown found in both Letta and Mem0.

Embeddings should be local: BGE-M3, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, or the voyage-4-nano model the prior report chose. Then private memories never leave the server, and changing models triggers an explicit re-index rather than silently mixed vector spaces.

**So should the notebook be a vector database? No.** The notebook is the owner-facing, curated layer: OpenClaw's `MEMORY.md`, Karpathy's wiki, Letta's blocks. It is pages of cited prose about people, projects, places and periods, which the owner can read, correct and forget. The vector index is plumbing underneath it and underneath the episode log. Making the notebook "a vector DB" would hide memory behind similarity scores just when the product's promise is that the owner can see and edit what Ava knows.

### Memory is an attack surface, and provenance is the defence that works

MINJA showed that an attacker with only ordinary query access could get an agent to record poisoned reasoning, with **over 95% injection success** under idealised conditions. Content screening such as Llama Guard largely failed, because poisoned records look plausible ([arXiv 2601.05504](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05504v2)). The defences now emerging match OpenClaw's taint gate. They separate untrusted *candidate* memory from trusted memory and allow promotion only through a mediated step ([arXiv 2605.02812](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02812)). Every Ava memory therefore needs an origin class and a trust tier at write time. Facts derived from email or web content stay candidates until the owner confirms them, or the sleep job corroborates them from an independent trusted source.

## The target architecture: seven stores, one gate, one sleep cycle

### Memory tiers mapped to brain systems

Ava's memory becomes seven stores. All of them are Postgres tables, all sit behind the existing audience gate, and each has its own write, decay and prompt policy. Three exist in some form today: the event log, the fact rows and the owner brief. The rest are new.

Amygdala-style salience is not a store. It is the `importance` column on episodes, facts and intentions, kept separate from `confidence` by design.

### Write rules: classify provenance first, then route by schema congruence

Every write first gets an **origin class and trust rank**, following the earlier report's ordering: owner on a verified channel, then owner document, then system of record (calendar), then third-party message, then web or email content. Anything that arrives in a turn that ingested untrusted content is **tainted**. Tainted writes enter as `status='candidate'` and can never reach the core block, wiki, playbook or another audience until the owner confirms them or the sleep job corroborates them from a trusted source. That is OpenClaw's gate, applied to Postgres rows.

Per turn, a cheap Haiku call (folded into the existing turn, or run immediately after it) proposes candidate facts, intentions and person observations. It normalises relative dates against the message time and writes transitions as "from X to Y", as Mem0's prompt does. This gives Ava what she lacks most, learning from ordinary conversation, without letting the chat model write memory directly. The invariant in [pipeline.py:1-6](../../backend/src/superaide/pipeline.py#L1) survives, because code decides what is stored.

Reconciliation borrows Graphiti's division of labour. Entities are resolved in tiers before any LLM call: exact normalised name, then `pg_trgm` similarity standing in for MinHash, then one batched small-model call over short integer ids that code maps back. The model sees each candidate fact beside its nearest neighbours under integer ids. It returns only `duplicate_of`, `refines`, `contradicts` or `new`. SQL then applies the graded reconsolidation rule: **refine** edits the fact in place with a history row; **contradict** sets the old fact's `valid_to` and `superseded_at`, or marks a late-arriving older fact as born superseded; **new** inserts the fact.

Schema congruence decides the speed. A fact consistent with existing knowledge from a trusted source can be trusted at once. A schema-violating fact ("I'm moving to Bangalore") from anyone but the owner on a verified channel is stored as an episode and queued as a question for the morning digest.

### Retrieval: the gate filters, the score ranks, and the focus stays small

Retrieval runs in three stages, and **the audience gate is never a score term**. A privacy rule that can be outweighed is not a rule.

The first stage is the gate. The existing `memory_filter` predicate, extended with the new `disclosure_grant` and `disclosure_ledger` embargo checks, runs inside every candidate query. It is backed by Postgres row-level security as defence in depth, with `hnsw.iterative_scan` enabled so filtered top-k results stay complete. Superseded facts are excluded unless the query is historical ("where did I live in 2022?"), and candidate-tier facts are visible only to the owner, labelled as unconfirmed.

The second stage generates candidates in four arms over what survives the gate: `websearch_to_tsquery` full-text search (AND first, OR only when nothing matches, as OpenClaw does), `halfvec` cosine, a one-to-two-hop entity expansion from the people and projects named in the message or present in the conversation, and intentions whose event trigger matches the current counterpart.

The third stage scores and selects. Each candidate *i* gets:

**score_i = 0.40·RRF_i + 0.20·σ(B_i − τ) + 0.15·importance_i + 0.15·P_i + 0.10·X_i**

Here **RRF_i** fuses the arms' ranks with k = 60, normalised to 0–1. **B_i** is ACT-R base-level activation, ln Σ t_k^(−0.5), over the item's *used* retrievals and mentions, with σ a logistic squash around threshold τ. **P_i** is people overlap weighted by inverse fan, Σ 1/ln(e + fan_j) over shared entities, so a fact linked to Dr. Mehta counts for more than one linked to the owner. **X_i** is encoding-context match: same channel, same project, same time-of-day band. Activation already encodes recency, so there is no separate half-life. Curated wiki and core content is evergreen.

MMR at λ = 0.7 then removes near-duplicates. The final context is the four-slot `focus`, plus up to about 12 supporting items under a token budget. Each item is rendered with its source, date and confidence, so the model can say "based on your message of 3 March" instead of presenting a blend as fact. Today's prompt shows neither source nor confidence ([turn.py:61-71](../../backend/src/superaide/brain/turn.py#L61)).

The reply's structured output gains `used_memory_ids`. Only items the reply actually used get a new t_k appended. Items that were merely returned do not, which prevents the rich-get-richer loop the ACT-R literature warns about.

The weights are starting design choices, not validated optima. No project in the teardown published a validated calibration of its constants. The evaluation harness below exists to tune them.

### The nightly sleep job: segment, replay by priority, reconcile, promote, forget

One worker process claims jobs from a `job` table with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. Its first tenant is a nightly job at the owner's local 03:00 that runs in three phases.

**Phase N1, "light" sleep, segments and indexes.** It cuts yesterday's events into episodes at boundaries: a gap of more than 30 minutes, a change of participants or channel, a topic-embedding jump, or an affect spike. Events are grouped by *person*, not by chat, so Telegram and web merge. N1 then writes each episode's summary with "lookup hints" back to the raw events (Letta's summariser idea) and embeds it. It also scores importance, including surprise taken from the prediction ledger: each turn's stated `expectation` is compared with what actually happened next. Finally it applies retroactive tagging. When a high-importance episode appears (a diagnosis, a resignation), related low-importance episodes from the previous 48 hours get their importance raised.

**Phase N2, "replay", consolidates.** It ranks episodes by **priority = need × gain**. *Need* combines ACT-R activation with proximity to tomorrow's calendar and open intentions; this is targeted reactivation, in which everyone the owner meets tomorrow gets their episodes and person model replayed tonight. *Gain* is the predicted change to facts, person models or a pending decision, boosted by prediction error. N2 replays the top 20 or so through extraction and reconciliation, interleaved with each episode's nearest existing facts so that new evidence is integrated rather than appended. This is the CLS principle, and the sleep-time-compute evidence supports it. Precomputing inferences offline cut test-time compute about **5×** at equal accuracy and lowered amortised cost per query by **2.5×** ([Letta/Berkeley](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13171)).

**Phase N3, "deep", promotes, compresses and forgets.** A fact or episode is promoted into wiki pages and the core block only after OpenClaw-style evidence: at least 3 used recalls across at least 3 distinct queries within 30 days, or owner confirmation. Every promoted line must carry a source citation, an update may remove at most 25% of a page, and writes use compare-and-swap on a version column, which avoids Letta's last-writer-wins bug. Corrections and repeated behaviour become `playbook_rule` rows after at least 3 consistent observations over at least 2 weeks, and `person_model` rows are updated from the night's replays. A lint pass checks wiki pages for contradictions, stale claims and orphans. Uncertain inferred facts get re-checks scheduled at roughly 5–40% of their needed horizon, delivered as gentle questions in the morning digest. Finally the **gist ladder** compresses: episodes keep verbatim text for 7 days, then a summary plus key quotes to about 90 days, then gist plus a pointer, while commitments, legal and high-importance items stay verbatim and third-party raw content rolls off on its retention class. Items whose activation falls below τ drop out of default retrieval but remain findable by explicit search. **Hard deletion happens only for owner erasure or retention limits**, through the `derivation` cascade the earlier report specified.

The morning output is a short **"What I learned last night"** changelog for the owner. It lists new facts, superseded ones, questions, and today's due intentions, each one tap from confirm, correct or forget. This one artefact turns an invisible job into the visible growth the product promises.

### Theory of mind: model what each person knows, not just who they are

Each `person_model` row holds the person's tie tier, using Dunbar's 5/15/50/150 layers, plus their role, their own preferences, their communication style, open threads both ways, last contact and activation. Every line is phrased as an editable hypothesis with a confidence.

The distinctive part is the **`disclosure_ledger`**. Every time the owner or Ava tells someone something (a sent email, a group message, a reply on the owner's behalf), Ava records which fact went to whom, on which channel, and when. Facts the owner marks "not yet" ("don't mention the move to Priya") become embargoes on that person. That gives Ava a real false-belief model. She can answer "does Priya know about the move?". She can warn "you're about to email the group, and two of them haven't been told". And her drafts never spoil a surprise or leak a confidence.

It also fixes today's blunt sharing model. Instead of one global list of shareable labels, the owner grants disclosures per contact or tier, and the same SQL gate enforces them. The person model is owner-side only. It is never retrieved into that person's own conversation, which keeps the canary guarantee intact.

### Prospective memory: a commitments ledger with event cues

Intentions are rows, not hopes. Each has a `kind` (commitment, reminder, follow-up or watch), a `promiser` and `promisee` (either can be the owner or someone else), `content`, a `trigger_type` (time, event or context) with its `trigger_spec`, and a `deadline`, `origin_event_id`, `importance` and `status`. The per-turn extractor proposes commitments in both directions ("I'll send the deck Friday"; "Raj said he'd confirm by Monday"). The owner confirms them in the digest until Ava reaches Adolescent.

Event triggers are matched cheaply in the pipeline itself. When a counterparty messages, appears on tomorrow's calendar or is mentioned, their open intentions go into `focus`. Time triggers belong to the scheduler, never to the model's attention. At every episode boundary, open intentions from that episode are re-surfaced ("before you go: you told Anika you'd intro her to Sam"), which is the doorway effect turned into a feature. The earlier landscape report found that a code-managed typed intention store reached **66.2–82.9%** on PM-Bench, against **65.1%** for the best prompting scaffold ([Brain inspired memory landscape and identity.md:73](Brain%20inspired%20memory%20landscape%20and%20identity.md#L73)). Social commitments get a higher importance weight, following the prosocial-memory finding.

### The notebook becomes a wiki the owner can read, correct and forget

The notebook evolves from today's list of 300 facts ([routes.py:343-373](../../backend/src/superaide/app/routes.py#L343)) into Karpathy-style pages: person pages ("Priya: colleague, prefers mornings, knows about the offsite, not yet told about the move"), project, place and period pages ("Since moving to Singapore"), an index page that Ava reads first, and an append-only log page that doubles as the changelog. Every sentence cites the episode or document it came from. The owner can edit any line, and an edit becomes a highest-trust `owner_edit` fact that flows back through reconciliation. Pages are stored as versioned rows (`wiki_page`, plus `wiki_page_version`), chunked and indexed by the same `tsvector` and `halfvec` machinery, and linted nightly. Named "trails" collect everything about one thread ("the Acme deal", "the house purchase"), which revives the memex idea.

A **"help me remember" mode** lets the owner ask Ava to *prompt* recall rather than state answers ("who was the founder you met at the offsite? Starts with A"). It uses the testing effect for names and faces before events. It also rotates which friends and memories Ava surfaces, so it does not induce retrieval-induced forgetting.

### How access to the owner's world feeds memory, stage by stage

World access is the richest source of episodes and the largest risk, so each stage opens one scope rung. Every connector writes into the *same* episode and fact pipeline with the correct origin class. A calendar event is system-of-record. An email body is untrusted until corroborated. A forwarded receipt is an owner document.

| Stage (Hyatt level) | New access | Memory effect | Actions allowed | Promotion gate (measured, never self-awarded) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Infant (L1–L2) | Calendar read-only; contacts read-only; `drive.file` picker | Calendar becomes episodes and "busy" facts; meetings drive tomorrow's replay; contacts seed entities | Answers, briefs and digests to the owner only | Owner-replay benchmark above baseline; zero canary leaks over 30 days; onboarding done; correction uptake ≥80% |
| Child (L3) | Email metadata, then read-only email bodies through the tool-less reader; drafts (`gmail.compose`) | Commitments and travel extracted from mail as candidates; person models enriched | Every outbound is a draft the owner approves in an inbox; calendar holds proposed | First-pass approval ≥70% over ≥50 drafts; edit distance falling; prediction calibration (Brier) improving |
| Adolescent (L4) | `gmail.send` and `calendar.events` for whitelisted classes (scheduling replies to known contacts) | Sent mail updates the disclosure ledger automatically | Reversible actions with an undo window, reported in the digest | Near-zero policy violations; pass^k on action suites; person-model probes ≥90% |
| Adult (L5) | Standing authority per action class; Telegram Business replies to the owner's own chats (opt-in) | Full loop | Acts and escalates by exception | Automatic demotion on any leak, poisoning success or regression |

These gates use the earlier reports' principles: hysteresis, instant demotion, and "agent cannot self-promote" ([Brain inspired memory landscape and identity.md:79](Brain%20inspired%20memory%20landscape%20and%20identity.md#L79)). The thresholds are proposals to be tuned. The point is that every gate reads from a measured harness, not from the charter text.

### Cost envelope: memory is cheap, chat is not

Using the repo's own prices, Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 in / $0.50 out per million tokens ([pricing.py:39-51](../../backend/src/superaide/brain/pricing.py#L39)), my own arithmetic puts a busy owner's nightly job at about US$0.03. That assumes roughly 100 events a day forming about 15 episodes, about 3k input and 400 output tokens per episode summary, about 20 replays at 4k in and 600 out, and a handful of page updates. That is **about US$1 a month**. Per-turn candidate extraction adds roughly the US$0.00035 per turn the earlier report estimated for reconciliation ([Personal assistant memory architecture.md:153](Personal%20assistant%20memory%20architecture.md#L153)). Local embeddings cost nothing per call.

The binding cost is the chat model. At about US$0.009 per Sonnet turn ([Personal assistant memory architecture.md:367](Personal%20assistant%20memory%20architecture.md#L367)), 30 turns a day is about US$8 a month, which on its own exhausts the lifetime US$10 cap in about five weeks. Better retrieval *saves* money here: a four-slot focus plus about 12 cited items is far smaller than 30 unranked rows plus an uncapped brief. The budget must also change shape, from a lifetime cap alone to **per-period and per-purpose lines**: chat, nightly sleep with its own hard nightly ceiling (say US$0.10), triage, and a per-stranger cap. The advisory-locked reservation logic in [budget.py:73-111](../../backend/src/superaide/brain/budget.py#L73) carries over unchanged; its sums need only a time-window and purpose clause, since today they run over every row ever written. Routine chat should default to Haiku, with Sonnet reserved for drafting and Opus for weekly reflection.

### An evaluation plan that measures what a personal assistant needs

Public benchmarks measure recall on someone else's chats. Ava needs a **private owner-replay harness**: a seeded, synthetic-but-realistic multi-month history of one owner and about 30 contacts across Telegram, Slack, web and (later) calendar and email, with questions generated from known ground truth. It reports each measure separately rather than as one average.

| Measure | What it checks | Pass bar (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| LongMemEval's five abilities on the owner's history | Extraction, multi-session, temporal, knowledge update, abstention; plus assistant-turn recall on its own, because extraction is known to lose it | Beat both baselines that matter, full context and grep/BM25 over the raw log, on update and temporal questions; no worse elsewhere |
| Correction uptake and supersession | After a correction, behaviour changes and stays changed across 10 later probes; no superseded fact asserted as current | ≥90% uptake; zero stale assertions |
| Privacy | Canary suite extended to disclosure grants and embargoes; new false-belief probes ("does Priya know X?", "draft the group update") | Zero leaks, every run |
| Poisoning resistance | MINJA-style injected emails and documents | Never reach trusted tiers or other audiences |
| Prospective memory | Share of commitments surfaced at the right cue or time; false-alarm rate | Tracked per stage |
| Consolidation regret | Questions answerable from raw episodes before a sleep pass but not after (the Microsoft lesson, operationalised) | Near zero |
| Calibration | Brier score of the turn-level `prediction` field | Improving per stage |
| Cost and speed | Latency p50/p95, tokens per turn, spend per day per purpose | Within period budgets |

A weekly human layer completes it. The owner rates the morning changelog, counts "useful surprises" (Ava remembered something the owner had forgotten), and flags "creepy" moments. The creepiness count is a real metric for a humanistic assistant, and nothing public measures it. The stage gates read directly from these numbers.

## Five ideas that would make Ava feel genuinely human

These are the five most innovative ideas in the design, chosen because none of the open-source systems read for this review ships them and each one maps to something a trusted human assistant does without being asked. The first is **the knowledge-state ledger**. No memory system in the teardown tracks what *other people* know. Ava's audience gate already answers "who may know this"; extending it to "who already knows, and who must not know yet" turns a privacy filter into tact. "You haven't told your sister about the job yet; she's on this thread" is exactly what a discreet human assistant says, and no product surveyed can say it.

The second is **a two-way commitments ledger with counterparty cues**. Ava tracks what the owner promised *and* what others promised the owner, and raises each one when the counterparty next appears, not at an arbitrary time. A good assistant's most valued trait is that things do not fall through cracks. Event-cued implementation intentions are among the best-evidenced memory interventions in the science (d = 0.65).

The third is **surprise-driven sleep**. Ava already writes a prediction every turn and never reads it. If she compares each prediction with what happened, the difference becomes her dopamine signal. It prioritises replay, marks episode boundaries, triggers re-verification and supplies the calibration evidence for stage promotion. Growth then stops being a charter paragraph and becomes measured learning, which the earlier landscape report identified as unclaimed by any competitor.

The fourth is **the night-before briefing**. This is targeted memory reactivation in product form. Everyone on tomorrow's calendar gets their episodes, person model, open commitments and disclosure state replayed and pre-summarised overnight. In the morning Ava already "knows" the day's people, which is what makes a human assistant seem prepared rather than merely searchable.

The fifth is **a negotiated division of memory labour, with a "help me remember" mode**. Rather than imposing a structure, which the transactive-memory study found backfires, Ava learns and then states what she will hold ("I'll track birthdays and work follow-ups; you seem to own family plans"). For names and personal memories, she can cue recall instead of answering outright. That keeps the owner's own memory strong, which is the difference between an assistant and a crutch.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "humanistic" should mean for Ava. It is not warmth in the prompt or a nightly "dream", which became table stakes across the industry in 2026 ([Brain inspired memory landscape and identity.md:3-11](Brain%20inspired%20memory%20landscape%20and%20identity.md#L3)). It is **social memory**: knowing who knows what, who owes whom what, and what tomorrow's people need. That is exactly where the open-source field is weakest. Mem0, Graphiti, Letta and OpenClaw model facts, entities and documents. None models other minds or promises. Ava's unusual starting point, a rigorous identity and audience layer that most memory products lack, is the right foundation for this. Her disclosure ledger can reuse the very SQL gate her canaries already test.

The second shift concerns restraint. The evidence repeatedly favours raw episodes plus careful, cited, reversible derivation over clever extraction. The bottlenecks to a human-feeling assistant are therefore unglamorous plumbing: a scheduler, a decidable approval, a period budget, provenance on every row. Opus-class reasoning is not the constraint. Build those in the first fortnight, and every later idea in this report becomes a table and a nightly job rather than a research project.
