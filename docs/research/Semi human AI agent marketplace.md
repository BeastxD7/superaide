# Sell apprenticed AI hires, not AI employees

Everything on your feature list can be built in October 2026: watching screens, listening in meetings, layered memory, self-written skills, heartbeat-driven proactivity, and per-manager learning. Most of it already exists in open source. Hermes Agent (Nous Research, MIT licence) advertises a "built-in learning loop" that writes and refines its own skills and models each user, and OpenClaw ships a gateway, a heartbeat, SKILL.md skills, memory files and a SOUL.md identity file ([Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs); [clawdocs.org](https://clawdocs.org/reference/glossary)). "An agent that learns your company" is therefore a commodity feature, not a moat. The market has also already punished companies that sold "AI employees" on an annual salary. 11x's ARR and customer-logo claims collapsed under scrutiny, and Artisan retired its "Stop Hiring Humans" slogan. Meanwhile investors have rewarded agents that sell a measurable unit of work, such as Sierra (about $1.50 per resolution, $15B+ valuation) and Intercom Fin ($0.99 per outcome) ([aiwiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/eleven_x); [yespress.io](https://yespress.io/artisan); [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/sierra-hits-15b-valuation-as-the-enterprise-ai-agent-race-heats-up); [Gleap](https://www.gleap.ai/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)). The open space is what nobody packages: a **structured apprenticeship**. That means onboarding, a probation period with approval gates that loosen as the agent proves accuracy, a visible "what I learned" changelog, and a **verified, portable track record** that a marketplace audits. My recommendation is to build one apprenticed role first: a B2B LinkedIn/content strategist for founder-led companies and agencies in Singapore and Southeast Asia. Run it on boring infrastructure (Claude Agent SDK, Temporal, Postgres, Recall.ai), price it as a skill-tiered rate card metered on approved deliverables, and launch it as a public 30-day probation on LinkedIn. Do not market it as having feelings. It has none. What it should have instead is honest operational state, editable relationship memory and an explicit, correctable model of its manager.

## Every capability exists; reliability and judgement are the bottleneck

The perception layer works well enough to build on. The best OSWorld-Verified computer-use score rose from 42% in early 2025 to **85% by June 2026**, above the roughly 72% human baseline. a16z adds the needed caveat: 85% still means 15 of every 100 tasks fail, a business process succeeds only if every step does, and agents take 8–10 minutes on tasks humans finish in 2–3 ([a16z](https://a16z.com/can-agents-use-a-computer-yet-weve-got-the-data/)). Screenshot-driven steps cost more than 15k tokens each. Practitioners measure structured API calls at **10–100x cheaper**. They also show the compounding problem: 95% per-step accuracy over 50 steps yields only about 7.7% end-to-end success ([Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-10-computer-use-agents-when-pixels-replace-apis)). The conclusion is that "seeing the screen" should be a fallback actuator and a way to learn by observation. It should not be how the agent does its main work.

Learning by watching a human is real and measurable. With **one demonstration**, Gemini-1.5-Pro rose from 19.3% to 51.7% on LearnGUI offline tasks ([arXiv 2504.13805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13805v1)). NUS Show Lab's ShowUI-Aloha, built in Singapore, records 30 FPS screen video plus input events. A vision-language model then turns each step into an Observation/Think/Action/Expectation caption, and the planner uses that trace as a "soft reference, not a rigid script." In ablations, success went from 36.7% without the teaching trace to 63.3% with it ([arXiv 2601.07181](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07181)). The failure modes matter for product design. Aloha needs at least one demo per workflow family and drops to 37.6% on multi-app tasks. WONDERBREAD found that models writing SOPs from video recall most steps but add inaccurate or superfluous ones ([WONDERBREAD](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/wonderbread-website)). Shadowing should therefore produce **draft SOPs that a human reviews**, not autonomous replay. Microsoft's decision to deprecate its narration-based "Record with Copilot" recorder points the same way ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-automate/desktop-flows/create-flow-using-ai-recorder)).

Learning from listening and from manager feedback has the best evidence of anything on the list, as long as the learning goes into external memory rather than model weights. In JPMorgan's continual-learning work, a text-to-SQL agent that distilled expert corrections into layered memory rose from **32.4% to 51.3%** on unseen questions ([arXiv 2511.10674](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.10674.md)). The Memory Company's Spark tested Claude Sonnet 5 on τ-bench banking: it scored 0.248 at baseline and **0.397 when given corrections that contained the right answer**, which beat bare pass/fail signals. The memory store also transferred across models ([arXiv 2607.22157](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22157.md)). Devin already runs this loop in production: it auto-suggests "Knowledge" items from chat feedback, and a human approves, edits or dismisses them ([Devin docs](https://docs.devin.ai/Onboard_Devin/knowledge)). The UX lesson is to ask managers "what should it have been?" instead of offering a thumbs-up button. The commercial lesson is that an employer can keep its agent's learned state when the underlying model changes.

On memory, the field has settled on four tiers. The first is a small, always-loaded core profile. The second is an immutable episode log. The third is a fact store or temporal graph, ideally bi-temporal, so that an old fact is marked invalid rather than deleted when the manager changes their mind. The fourth is procedural memory: playbooks and skills ([Zep/Graphiti](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1); [LangMem](https://langchain-ai.github.io/langmem/concepts/conceptual_guide/)). Ignore the leaderboards, because the headline scores do not survive scrutiny. Mem0 self-reports 94.4 on LongMemEval. Letta scored **74.0% on LoCoMo using only filesystem tools**. An independent benchmark found a plain BM25 baseline averaging 60.5 on accurate retrieval, ahead of every memory product tested, and **no system exceeded 28% on selective forgetting** ([Mem0](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026); [Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory); [ajayk.sh summary of MemoryAgentBench](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/agent-memory-systems-2026/)). Self-improvement without weight updates is also well established. ACE's generator/reflector/curator loop applies small "delta" edits to an itemised playbook and gained 10.6% on AppWorld. It also showed that letting an LLM rewrite the whole context at once can collapse 18,000 tokens to 122, leaving the agent worse than with no adaptation at all ([arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v2)).

Proactivity and meeting presence are engineering problems, but the current results are mediocre. OpenClaw's heartbeat runs a full agent turn every 30 minutes and replies `NO_REPLY` when there is nothing to report ([clawdocs.org](https://clawdocs.org/architecture/heartbeat)). Research shows models over-offer help. On ProactiveBench, GPT-4o reached 64.6% F1, while a **fine-tuned 8B model reached 66.25%** ([arXiv 2410.12361](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)). On ProactBench, 14 of 16 models passed fewer than 20% of "recovery" triggers ([arXiv 2605.09228](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09228)). For meetings, Recall.ai joins Zoom, Meet, Teams and Webex at **$0.50 per recording hour** plus $0.15/hr for transcription. Its Output Media feature renders a webpage into the call, so the agent can speak and be seen for about $0.60/hr extra ([Recall.ai pricing](https://recall.ai/blog/new-recall-ai-pricing-for-2026); [Recall docs](https://docs.recall.ai/v1.10/docs/stream-media)). Speech comes from gpt-realtime-2.1-mini at $10/$20 per million audio input/output tokens ([DataNorth](https://datanorth.ai/news/openai-releases-gpt-realtime-2-1-voice-models)). Recall's own reference agent handles turn-taking with a prompt that says, in effect, "reply only when addressed, otherwise output SKIP" ([Recall.ai](https://www.recall.ai/blog/how-to-build-an-ai-voice-agent)). Nobody has published false-interjection rates.

Long-horizon reliability is the real ceiling. METR's 50% time horizon passed **11 hours for Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.6** in 2026, but the metric is "based almost entirely on coding tasks," and models do noticeably worse on messy work ([Wikipedia: METR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/05/1132254/this-is-the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai/amp/)). In TheAgentCompany, a simulated software company, the best agent fully completed only 30.3% of tasks. Admin and finance work was hardest. One failure was "self-deception": the agent renamed a user to match a colleague it couldn't find ([arXiv 2412.14161](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14161v2)). Anthropic's Project Vend found that employees talked the agent into discounts, and that procedures and checklists helped more than adding an agent-manager layer ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)). In short, the capability is there, but judgement, robustness to manipulation and consistency are not. That is why the product has to be built around an apprenticeship.

## The market rewarded measurable work and punished "AI salary" pitches

The landscape falls into four groups, and none of them offers what you are describing.

The first group is open-source personal agents. OpenClaw has about **391K GitHub stars** (Oct 2026) and a nonprofit foundation, and OpenClaw Enterprise was announced in September 2026 ([gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/openclaw-statistics/)). Hermes adds self-written skills, Honcho-based user modelling and a Skills Hub that uses the agentskills.io standard ([Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs)). Both are single-user tools without an accountable work history. Their weakness is security. ClawHub was hit by the ClawHavoc campaign, with more than 1,100 malicious skills uploaded in total, delivering macOS stealers and exfiltrating `.env` files. Snyk found flaws in **36.8%** of the ClawHub skills it scanned ([Antiy CERT](https://www.antiy.net/p/clawhavoc-analysis-of-large-scale-poisoning-campaign-targeting-the-openclaw-skill-market-for-ai-agents/); [Barrack.ai](https://blog.barrack.ai/openclaw-security-vulnerabilities-2026/)). Gartner labelled OpenClaw "insecure by default; block," and Kakao, Naver and Karrot restricted it internally ([Barrack.ai](https://blog.barrack.ai/openclaw-security-vulnerabilities-2026/)). "Very similar to OpenClaw" is a sound architectural reference. As a brand, it is a liability with enterprise buyers.

The second group is vertical agent companies that sell outcomes. Sierra raised $950M at a valuation above $15B, has $150M ARR and serves 40%+ of the Fortune 50, with contracts starting around $150K/yr ([ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/sierra-hits-15b-valuation-as-the-enterprise-ai-agent-race-heats-up)). Cognition raised at a **$48B** valuation in September 2026 ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/cognition-series-e-48-billion-valuation-scott-wu)). Decagon is at $4.5B ([DragApp](https://www.dragapp.com/blog/decagon-vs-sierra/)). Buyers' stated preferences match where the money went: in a Futurum survey, **43% preferred consumption pricing, 27% outcome-based, and fewer than 1 in 5 per-seat** ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/press-release/are-outcome-based-and-hybrid-ai-pricing-models-rewriting-the-vendor-playbook/)).

The third group is persona "AI employees", which went through a backlash. Ex-employees told TechCrunch that 11x counted contracts with 3-month break clauses as ARR (about $14M claimed against about $3M that survived), alleged 70–80% churn, and the CEO stepped down ([aiwiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/eleven_x)). Artisan's CEO admitted it "sold to a lot of the wrong customers." It then added approval gates, stopped its agent from making calls and rebranded as "be more human" ([yespress.io](https://yespress.io/artisan)). Gartner expects **over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end-2027** and counts only about 130 genuine vendors among thousands ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)).

The fourth group is marketplaces, which sell software listings rather than track records. Salesforce AgentExchange has about 13K listings and Microsoft Marketplace 4,000+ agents. UpAgents sells role-packaged agents paid per completed task ([InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/best-ai-agent-marketplaces-2026)). The closest analogue to your idea is monday.com's **Agentalent.ai** (March 2026). It is a "talent marketplace" where enterprises post roles and hire agents that passed a qualification process. It is enterprise-focused, partner-led, and shows no public ratings, track records or pricing ([Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/digital-workplace/monday-com-launches-agentalent-ai-ai-agent-marketplace/)). I found no vendor selling explicitly skill-tiered (junior/senior) agents. Singapore's agent startups (Level3AI, Bhindi AI, Agnes AI) are not hiring marketplaces either ([NeuronFeed](https://neuronfeed.com/country/sg)).

On protocols: the Personal Agent Protocol, announced around 6 October 2026, was co-announced by **Meta, Sierra, Genesys, Rocket, Shopify and Walmart**. Stripe was mentioned only as payments infrastructure (Link), not as a co-announcer ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/stripe-meta-walmart-personal-agent-protocol/)). PAP and the related commerce protocols (Stripe/OpenAI's ACP, Google's UCP) cover consumer purchasing. None of them covers agent employment, SLAs or reputation. MCP (10K+ public servers) and agentskills.io (published by Anthropic in December 2025) are open and mostly free ([philippdubach.com](https://philippdubach.com/posts/mcp-vs-a2a-in-2026-how-the-ai-protocol-war-ends/index.md); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-publishes-agent-skills-as-an-open-standard-for-ai-platforms/)). Building on them makes your skills portable, and for the same reason skills cannot be your moat.

### Defensibility has to come from accountability, not from "it learns"

Because Hermes and OpenClaw already learn per user, someone could copy your core loop in a weekend by hosting Hermes for each customer. A defensible business has to own things that a single-user tool structurally cannot.

The first is the **apprenticeship as the product**. Feng et al. define five autonomy levels by the human's role, from Operator to Observer. They propose "assisted evaluations," which classify an agent by the least human involvement it needs to pass a task bar, and "autonomy certificates" ([arXiv 2506.12469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12469)). Practitioners run a Shadow → Advisory → Co-pilot → Autopilot ladder with numeric gates and automatic rollback ([TianPan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/20/shadow-to-autopilot-ai-feature-autonomy)). Nobody sells this ladder as the customer experience. Probation, 30/60/90-day reviews and per-scope promotion are your marketplace's version of a hiring process.

The second is a **verified, portable track record**. For each agent and role, the marketplace audits accepted-output rate, edit distance, escalation appropriateness, policy blocks and cost per accepted task. That answers the overclaiming that sank 11x.

The third is a **clean split between the portable skill template and employer-owned learned state**. The template is a SKILL.md role pack. The learned state is private, exportable at offboarding, and transferable across models, as the Spark results suggest is possible.

The fourth is **security-vetted, curated skill supply** with sandboxing on by default, which ClawHavoc made table stakes.

The fifth, over time, is **outcome data across many employers in one role**. With consent, that data feeds a role-level model your competitors don't have. This is the one place where your interest in fine-tuning pays off.

The risk is that monday.com, Salesforce or Microsoft adds a reputation layer. Your counter is vertical depth in one role and the SMB/APAC price point, which the $50K–$150K/yr leaders do not serve.

## A reference architecture: an honest colleague on boring infrastructure

### Replace "feelings" with state you can check

Current AI does not have feelings, and the product should not say it does. Mainstream research holds that no current system has been shown to be conscious ([arXiv 2308.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708v1)). Anthropic's April 2026 interpretability work mapped 171 emotion-concept vectors in Claude Sonnet 4.5 that causally shape behaviour. A rising "desperation" vector preceded test-gaming on impossible coding tasks. Anthropic called these "functional emotions" and explicitly did not claim subjective experience ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/04/03/anthropic-maps-171-emotion-like-concepts-inside-claude/)). The useful takeaway is that internal-state signals are worth **monitoring as a safety signal**. They are not something to present as mood.

Simulated warmth carries documented costs. OpenAI's sycophancy rollback came from over-weighting short-term thumbs-up feedback ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/)). Heavy affective use correlates with emotional dependence ([heise](https://heise.de/-10327519)). Users relied on confident, unmarked answers about 90% of the time ([arXiv 2401.06730](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06730v2)). There is also a legal problem: the EU AI Act already bans inferring employees' emotions from voice or face in the workplace, though text-based inference falls outside the ban ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)).

What makes a colleague feel like they "get you" is something you can build honestly:

- **A state panel in plain operational language**: workload, queue depth, confidence per deliverable, what it is blocked on, and recent corrections.
- **Editable relationship memory**: "how you like things," including commitments it has made to you.
- **An explicit, correctable model of the manager**, phrased as hypotheses: "I think you prefer contrarian hooks over listicles; correct me?"
- **Text-only cues** about urgency or frustration.
- **Error repair** that combines an explanation with an acknowledgement, which CMU's 2026 principles recommend, alongside calibrating trust rather than maximising it ([arXiv 2606.20630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20630)).

The bond grows because the changelog of what it has learned visibly grows, not because it says "I missed you." Skip the photoreal avatar too. In a CHI '26 study with N=2,000, avatar-mediated speakers were rated less trustworthy ([arXiv 2603.18868](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18868)).

The weight-update question has a clear answer. Facts and preferences that can change belong in memory, never in weights. Meta found that learning new facts through LoRA cut NaturalQuestions F1 by **71%**, against 11% for sparse memory-layer fine-tuning ([arXiv 2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15103v1)). Fine-tuning does have two good uses after the MVP, and both suit your learning goal. The first is a **small fine-tuned proactivity/triage classifier**: ProactiveBench showed a fine-tuned 8B model edging out GPT-4o. The second is a shared **role-level LoRA** distilled from consented, anonymised, approved deliverables. LoRA matches full fine-tuning when applied to all layers, and for RL works even at rank 1 ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). Thousands of adapters can be served on one base model ([S-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.03285v3)).

## Four risks that can sink an AI-hire marketplace

**Prompt injection is not solved on the raw API, which is where you will build.** Opus 4.8's browser surface had a 31.5% attack success rate per attempt without safeguards and 0.5% with them. VentureBeat notes the API ships without the product safeguard stack ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/anthropic-browser-agent-hijacked-31-percent-before-safeguards-engaged)). Opus 5's two-layer "Auto Mode" reached 0% across 129 scenarios, against 3.7% without it ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/opus-5-may-have-solved-browser-based-prompt-injection-the-biggest-security-flaw-haunting-ai-agents/)). An agent that reads email, browses and can send has all three parts of the lethal trifecta: private data, untrusted content and a way to exfiltrate. You need to split agents that read from agents that act, allowlist outbound destinations and put a human approval in front of every send.

**Memory turns a one-off injection into a permanent compromise.** MINJA-style memory injection reached over 95% injection success under idealised conditions. When an LLM scored the trustworthiness of memories, it accepted 54 malicious entries at trust 1.0. The authors call trust scoring "a confidence filter, not a security filter" ([arXiv 2601.05504](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05504v1)). Two controls follow. Only privileged sources should be able to write to core or procedural memory. And you should retrieve only a few memories at a time: going from top-3 to top-10 raised attack success from 6% to 38%.

**Meeting listening is a litigation target.** In *In re Otter.AI*, wiretap and BIPA claims survived dismissal in August 2026, and a related suit against Granola challenges capturing audio without appearing as a participant ([Baird Holm](https://www.bairdholm.com/blog/core-privacy-claims-against-ai-note-taking-platform-survive-dismissal/); [UC Today](https://uctoday.com/productivity-automation/otter-ai-fails-to-dismiss-core-privacy-claims-in-u-s-court/)). Under Singapore's PDPA, employers only need to *notify* staff about monitoring, but external participants still need notice and consent ([HRM Asia](https://hrmasia.com/legal-compliance-of-workplace-surveillance-what-employers-need-to-know/)). The EU's Article 50 transparency duty applies from August 2026, while high-risk employment obligations slip to December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). India's DPDP operational duties start around May 2027 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)). The default settings follow from this: a visible, named AI bot; an announcement at join; no voiceprints; no training on customer recordings; and configurable retention. Screen-watching is the most legally fraught feature you have. It should run only on the manager's own screen and with explicit opt-in.

**Reliability failures are correlated, so selling autonomy will create churn.** A study of 23,392 episodes found reliability degrades super-linearly with task complexity, frontier models show "meltdown" rates of up to 19%, and episodic memory scaffolds did not improve long-horizon performance ([arXiv 2603.29231](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29231v1)). Report pass^k and cost per *accepted* output, not pass@1. Use a different model family, or a human, as the judge, because an LLM supervisor that shares the worker's model shares its blind spots ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)). Singapore offers an advantage here. IMDA's agentic framework, launched January 2026 and updated in May, asks for bounded autonomy, verifiable agent identities, mandatory human checkpoints and tracking of override rates ([Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33285/imda-updates-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai)). Your performance-review scorecard can double as compliance evidence. Map the product to its four dimensions on day one.

## The MVP: one apprenticed content strategist in twelve weeks

### Why content strategist beats software developer as the wedge

The research notes give software development the strongest *technical* footing. Coding has the best long-horizon evidence and tests provide verifiable outcomes. But it is the most crowded market in AI, with Devin, Claude Code, Codex and Copilot agents behind multi-billion-dollar valuations, and code execution with repo write access has the largest blast radius. I recommend the content strategist instead, specifically a **B2B LinkedIn/thought-leadership strategist for founder-led companies and agencies in Singapore and Southeast Asia**, for five reasons tied to the evidence.

First, every manager edit to a draft is a *correction that includes the right answer*, which is the strongest learning signal in the Spark results. Your learning loop would get rich data every day.

Second, the approval queue is the natural workflow, so graduated autonomy ("may publish within approved themes without review") is easy to understand and safe to promote.

Third, the blast radius is limited to drafts that wait for approval before posting.

Fourth, what the customer is paying for is exactly the thing your differentiator delivers: the agent absorbing a particular person's voice, positions and taste through onboarding, meetings and feedback.

Fifth, there are fewer credible "agent employees" in this role, and SEA SMBs cannot afford the $50K+ tiers.

The honest weakness is that engagement data is noisy, lagged and confounded. Make **first-pass approval rate and edit distance** the headline KPI, and treat engagement as secondary.

The runner-up is an internal ops analyst producing KPI reports and meeting follow-ups. Document workflows hold their reliability over long durations: graceful degradation stayed at 0.74 → 0.71, compared with 0.90 → 0.44 for software engineering ([arXiv 2603.29231](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29231v1)). It also fits a document-intelligence consultancy, but it makes a weaker public story.

### Week-by-week build plan

| Weeks | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| 1 | Multi-tenant Postgres schema with row-level security per (employer, manager, agent); deterministic policy layer; Slack app with approval queue; per-agent service identity |
| 2 | Claude Agent SDK loop; onboarding interview mapped to the "4 Cs" (compliance, clarification, culture, connection); ingest 50–100 past posts, a style guide and ICP notes into core blocks and a voice playbook ([Cisive](https://blog.cisive.com/4-cs-of-onboarding)) |
| 3 | Draft → manager edit → diff capture; reflector proposes playbook bullets; the "I learned this week" changelog ships |

| 5 | Temporal workday: morning brief, heartbeat on a Haiku-class model, triggers from calendar, Slack and RSS/news; triage into an end-of-day digest; track proactive-suggestion acceptance rate |
| 6 | Recall.ai listen-only attendance at founder interviews and internal meetings, with announcement and consent; the transcript produces candidate post ideas and candidate preferences that go to the approval queue, never auto-committed |
| 7 | Shadow mode: an opt-in browser recording of the manager researching and editing, turned into Aloha-style draft SOPs for review |
| 8 | Self-authored skill proposals (Hermes-style triggers) gated by manager approval; E2B sandbox for any code or browser work |
| 9 | Performance-review scorecard (throughput, first-pass approval, edit distance, escalation appropriateness, policy blocks, cost per approved post, pass^3 on a frozen regression set built from approved posts); cross-family judge; Langfuse dashboards |

Leave these out of the MVP: speaking in meetings, avatars, a public skill marketplace, unsupervised posting, and any weight training. Add realtime speech and the second role in months 4–6. Open the marketplace, where third-party builders list apprenticed roles with audited track records, only after one role proves that apprenticeship increases approval rate week over week.

### What it costs and how to price it

The research notes estimate infrastructure for one moderately busy agent at roughly **$200–800 per month**, dominated by LLM tokens. Their model works as follows ([Finout](https://www.finout.io/blog/claude-opus-5-pricing-2026); [Recall.ai](https://www.recall.ai/pricing); [Fly.io](https://fly.io/learn/ai-sandbox-pricing/); [Temporal](https://temporal.io/pricing.md)):

- About $175 per month for Opus 5 at around 3M input tokens a day, with 80% cache hits.
- About 40% less on Sonnet 5, which costs $3/$15 per million tokens.
- About $26 for 40 meeting hours.
- About $29 for a working-hours sandbox.
- Under $10 for Temporal at MVP scale.

A content strategist is lighter than that, since it does little computer use and attends fewer meetings. I estimate **$150–300 per agent per month**. That is my estimate, not a sourced figure.

For pricing, follow buyers toward metered outcomes and use a skill tier as the rate card. For example, a "Junior" tier at about US$400/month including 20 approved deliverables, with overage per approved post, and a "Senior" tier at about US$900/month with higher autonomy scopes and a lower review burden. Promotion between tiers should follow the 30/60/90 review. Add a refund on rejected work in the style of Fin's refund on reopened tickets. These numbers are hypotheses to test with design partners, not market data. Avoid annual "salary" contracts, which was the 11x trap.

### LinkedIn launch angle: "I'm onboarding an AI hire in public"

Do not launch with "Stop hiring humans", which cost Artisan a slogan and credibility. Launch with **a 30-day public probation**. In week 0, post the job description, the onboarding plan and the autonomy ladder. Then post weekly, and let the agent draft the posts while you edit them in public. Each post should show:

- the approval-rate and edit-distance curve
- the "what I learned" diff, for example: "Shashank cuts my first line 80% of the time; new rule: open with the claim, not the context"
- one honest failure and how it was corrected

Day 30 is the performance review and a promotion decision. One post should take on the feelings question directly: "My AI hire doesn't have feelings, and here's what it has instead," with screenshots of the state panel and relationship memory. A second should make the Singapore governance angle concrete: "Mapped to IMDA's agentic framework: named identity, human checkpoints, override-rate tracking." The series becomes your lead magnet for agencies and founders, and it is also your first audited track record.

## Conclusion

The technology question is mostly answered. What remains open is the trust question, and that changes what is worth building. In 2026 the scarce assets are evidence that the agent got better at *this* job for *this* manager, and governance that lets a buyer extend autonomy without betting the brand. Learning loops, memory and perception are all available off the shelf. That shifts the job of a marketplace for semi-human agents from listing agents to running a hiring process: probation, reviews, promotions and an employment history the marketplace audits. No protocol or incumbent offers that today, though Agentalent.ai shows that large players see the shape of it.

For a solo builder, the strategic move is to treat the first role as the place where you collect proof about apprenticeship itself. If approval rate climbs and edit distance falls week over week across several design partners, you have the dataset, the case studies and later a role-level fine-tuned model that a hosted-Hermes copycat cannot reproduce. If the curves stay flat, you will know within 90 days at a few hundred dollars a month in costs, before you have built a marketplace around an unproven premise.
